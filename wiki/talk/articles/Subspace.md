Regardless, I would think that light and EM radiation, as well as other forms of radiation, would pass through from this layer more slowly, and so when interacting with normal space, would appear with a lag time. == More on slow... ==

See, my thinking is that when you are talking about time slowing down, everything you do is perceived as taking longer to do by the people in the "normal" time.  So, if I am in Subspace and I fire a weapon towards normal space it will seem like (to me) that it gets faster as it exits subspace.  That's how I perceived it.

== Slow vs. Normal... ==

The way I see it, light and energy slow, and so does time.  Time within the ship passes normally, as it is cut off from subspace, existing within a field that allows it (being matter) to stay there without being immediately ejected into normal space.  
Thus, if light slows, and energy and time slow, then it takes longer for things to traverse from one dimension to another, for light to travel, etc.
I'm not sure of the exact lag time, but even a second of lag is significant when firing at a far distance in space, or trying to track a mass shadow.  

Speaking of mass shadows, I would actually think it's the opposite of how we have it listed.  I should think that things in normal space create shadows in subspace, since their matter itself cannot exist, but can exert a gravitational force on subspace.  Ships in subspace would, I should think, not have any shadow at all, since the shadow should be in subspace, but it's not because the matter itself is there.  In normal space, I should think the only way to track subspace-traveling vessels is by either looking into subspace with your own stuff (how Ixoarchada Subspace arrays work), or by looking for the gamma radiation interactions in any plane.
