= Part 1 =

June 22, [[2316]]

by Seth Dickinson

(rewrite)

=====Titanian 11 - Titanian System - 1300 Hours=====
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The TDF asked for reinforcements from the Meroniri, Ikronin, and Lorithos. All of them promised to ‘lend assistance,’ but not for at least another six months. Gearing up for an interstellar military operation was time-consuming work.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The next six months of the war, from June to December, saw some of the most frequent, bloody fleet engagements of the war.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In late June, Scott Sanburn deployed one of his most reliable Admirals, Gordon Dannins, in an attempt to bring to the fight to the Imperials and relieve the siege of the inner planets. Dannins maneuvered his ships out of the Titanian system’s ecliptic plane, effectively ‘leapfrogging’ over Marshall’s Atlas Wing, and attacked Cronus.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dannins had at his disposal five ADF-5000 cruisers and seven ADF-800 fighter squadrons, a significant fraction of the TDF’s remaining assets. He was met by a garrison of ten Vigilant cruisers with fighter escort.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The TDF attack also had the element of surprise. Dannins charged in from the planetary north, catching the Imperial ships in low orbit. Particle beams ignited a brilliant aurora in the planet’s high atmosphere as the TDF cruisers pounded their surprised Imperial opponents.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Imperial carrier Exsanguinator suffered a drive hit and her orbit began to decay; she crashed into Titanian 12 an hour later, unable to jump away so deep in its gravity well.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The remaining Imperials accelerated out of orbit, closing up their formation. Dannins led his cruisers after them, freeing up his fighters to attack orbital facilities and Imperial infrastructure. The advanced ADF-800s quickly overwhelmed the Ravens defending the orbital yards and supply depots, and began strafing runs on their targets.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Imperial cruiser squadron suddenly stopped accelerating and activated their plasma shields, catching Dannins off-guard. The TDF ships didn’t begin braking in time to intercept the Imperials, and overshot them, suffering several MAC hits and inflicting little damage on the shielded Vigilants. The ADF-5000 Rickover was so badly mauled that Dannins ordered it to retreat.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Encouraged, the Imperials reactivated their drives to catch up with the ADF ships. This was a mistake. Dannins, farther from the planet, had plenty of room to maneuver; the Imperials found themselves flying into the teeth of his squadron’s heavy PACs.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A swift and savage medium-range gun duel, where the Imperials had the advantage, left Dannins’ flagship Haven wounded. He ordered his flotilla to close with the Imperials, and the gun duel transitioned into a close-range engagement. Here the ADF-5000’s turreted armaments gave them superiority; they could maneuver away from the Vigilants’ MAC guns while still bringing their own armament to bear.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;An hour later two Imperial cruisers had been destroyed; the remainders were either crippled, badly damaged, or out of MAC rounds. But tragedy had struck the ADF forces. A point-blank MAC shot had pierced Haven’s damaged shields and armor, blasting through her flag bridge and killing Admiral Dannins and his command crew instantly. The two sides disengaged.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Technically, the TDF task force had suffered no capital-ship losses, but a dark cloud hung over them as they returned to Cronus, gathered their fighters, and began the long journey back to Theia.


= Part 2 =
July 20, [[2316]]

by Seth Dickinson

(rewrite)

=====Titanian 11 - Titanian System - 1300 Hours=====
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Although the public mourned the loss of Admiral Dannins, his raid on Titanian 12 had been successful, and had given the TDF a chance of holding out until reinforcements arrived. Robert Marshall had plenty of ships for a final attack, but Dannins had destroyed many of the supplies that he needed for a successful offensive.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Marshall was forced to delay his attack once more, and he had to shift more of his ships back to Titanian 12 and 11 to garrison them. Although Anthony Hadrian, supreme commander of Imperial forces, forbade him from attacking Titanian 9 until everything was ready, Marshall launched a ‘reconnaissance in force’ with most of Atlas Wing on July 20.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Scott Sanburn had no choice but to meet him at Titanian 9. Thanks to Imperial raids, only fifteen ADF-5000s were ready for battle; to bolster his forces Sanburn scraped together every fighter he could find, even near-obsolete ADF-F-400s.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Fifty Vigilants from Atlas Wing approached Titanian 9 on sub-light drives at 0400 hours, July 20th. Sanburn hadn’t had any time to plan tactics or maneuvers. Both sides knew this battle was going to be a bloodbath; Sanburn dreaded the impending slaughter, but Marshall looked forward to the fighting with anticipation.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Marshall and his enormous wall of battle encountered Sanburn’s fighter-heavy force near the Lagrange point between Titanian 9 and one of its moons. Sanburn held his capital ships back, knowing that the massed firepower of so many MAC guns would shatter them.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Marshall ordered a full-out attack, but the sheer numbers of TDF fighters forced his cruisers back before they got into MAC range of the ADF-5000s. Reluctantly, he ordered his ships to breach the TDF fighter screen.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Squadrons of Ravens and Valkyries launched a full-out assault on the TDF screen. Human history had never witnessed a dogfight of such astounding proportions; nearly a thousand TDF fighters and five hundred Imperials, supported by fifty Vigilant cruisers, swarmed through space between Titanian 9 and its moon. The exchange of fire was so furious that Scott Sanburn, thousands of kilometers away onboard the Liberator, noted that he could see the dogfight as clearly as the sun.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Somewhere in this swirling miasma of fire and death, Emil Torsc of the elite Scimitar Squadron encountered Helen Cashaw, the greatest TDF ace. They dueled for the next thirty-five seconds, and the encounter ended in a head-to-head run, the ultimate test of opposing pilot’s nerves, reflexes, and will. Cashaw lost her nerve and fired too soon, chewing a wing off Torsc’s Raven. Ever calm, he put a cannon-burst through her cockpit canopy, killing her.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Although individual aerospace duels often lasted mere seconds, the overall struggle lasted two hours. Marshall had drilled his capital-ship crews on supporting fighters in a dogfight. The Vigilants had pre-planned fire lanes which Imperial fighters knew to avoid; TDF ships that strayed into these regions were pulped by sheer volume of Autocannon fire.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In the end Marshall’s preparation won the day over TDF grit, bravery, and numbers. TDF losses skyrocketed as Imperial fighters drew their opponents into the fire lanes, then let Atlas Wing cruisers finish them off. The TDF screen collapsed. Marshall’s wall of battle broke through and charged Sanburn’s cruisers. Sanburn ordered a general retreat. While his ADF-5000s were recovering their fighters, Marshall managed to hit two of the cruisers with overwhelming firepower, destroying both.
