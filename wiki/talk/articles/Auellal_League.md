Andrew, an official language doesn't mean the government forces people to speak it. It means they make public announcements, print official government documents, and hold government proceedings in that language.

For example, in the Prefecture the official languages are English and Latin. That only means government business is conducted in those languages. Local populations speak Mandarin, Gaelic, Hindi, Arabic, and a number of other dialects for local business. English is the trade language, Latin is the government language.

I think the Auellal should adopt an official language for government business, if only to save money on translators and printing costs! (haha)
