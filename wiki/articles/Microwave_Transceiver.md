For orbit/surface communications, many designs use tight-beam microwaves to carry information.

Microwaves can be intercepted, so commanders use two methods of encryption for their information. First, for the most important and sensitive orders, they use One Time Pad (OTP) encryption. OTP is theoretically unbreakable, but no Pad (base for encryption/decryption) can be used more than once. Surface commanders and orbiting commanders must periodically ensure that they have identical Pads, and agree on an order for use. These Pads are carried by armed courier from surface to orbit. If there's any possibility that Pad security has been compromised, the entire batch is destroyed and replaced.

Second, for less vital orders, they use Vernam ciphers. Vernam ciphers use a random encryption method that requires a key. Like the OTP system, keys are sent to battlefield commanders beforehand, but new ones are transmitted via OTP encryption rather than armed courier.

The Microwave Transceiver is the most common use of orbit/surface and surface/surface communications. The range is determined by the wattage of the transmitter, and the clarity of the signal (and resistance to jamming) is determined by the frequency.

Typically, military communications ranges from 25-115GHz (though dedicated stations can make it to 500GHz), and their power output depends on the size of the unit, from small 1mW infantry transmitters to 600gW planetary broadcast stations.
