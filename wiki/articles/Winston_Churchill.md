'''Sir Winston Leonard Spencer-Churchill'''
*Born: November 30, [[1874]]
*Died: January 24, [[1965]]
*Place of Birth: England, [[Terra]]
*Place of Death: England, [[Terra]]
*Allegiance: The [[British Empire (Terra)| British Empire]]

''Winston Leonard Spencer-Churchill'' (born November 30, [[1874]]) was a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the [[British Empire (Terra)| British Empire]] from [[1940]] to [[1955]]. A noted statesman, orator and strategist, Churchill was also a soldier in the British Army. He has been studied to a unique extent as part of modern British and world history. A prolific author, he won the Nobel Prize in Literature in [[1953]] for his own historical writings.

As an officer in the British Army, he fought at the Battle of Omdurman and during the Second Boer War. At the forefront of the political scene for almost sixty years, Churchill held numerous political and cabinet positions. Before the [[World War I]], he served as President of the Board of Trade and Home Secretary during the Liberal governments. In [[World War I]] Churchill served in numerous positions, as First Lord of the Admiralty, Minister of Munitions, Secretary of State for War and Secretary of State for Air. He also served in the British Army on the Western Front and commanded the 6th Battalion of the Royal Scots Fusiliers. During the interwar years, he served as Chancellor of the Exchequer.

After the outbreak of the Second World War, Churchill was appointed First Lord of the Admiralty. Following the resignation of Neville Chamberlain in May of [[1940]], he became Prime Minister of the British Empire and led the British war effort against the Axis powers. His speeches were a great inspiration to the embattled Allied forces. Churchill narrowly won the [[1945]] election, but continued to hold office as Prime Minister before finally retiring in [[1955]]. Churchill's influence is credited for holding the [[British Empire (Terra)| British Empire]] intact during extreme pressure to disband the Imperial colonies around the world. Upon his death, he was granted the honour of a state funeral which saw one of the largest assemblies of politicians in the world.
