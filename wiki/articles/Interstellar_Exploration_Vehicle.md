==History==

The Interstellar Exploration Vehicle was first dreamed up at the [[United Earth Alliance]] Space Agency during the wild heyday of space exploration. Yuri Krenschov had cracked the door open into [[hyperspace]], and the [[Terrans]] were determined to see what was out there.

The unified government's resources had boosted the [[Orion Spacecraft]] into an extremely effective vehicle for exploring [[Terra]]'s local neighbors, but it lacked the life support capacity for the lengthy voyages planned by the Space Agency.

===Alpha Centauri Flight===
On July 16, [[2018]], the Space Agency formed the Alpha Centauri Flight Commission. Their duties were to develop a ship that could support a crew on the 8 year round trip voyage to [[Alpha Centauri]], and include landing craft and probes to survey the star system for inhabitable planets.

The already massively successful [[Orion Spacecraft]]'s Planetary Surface Access Module was designated for use as the landing craft.

The blueprints for the IEV itself were drafted in November of [[2018]].

===Stellar Navigation and the Dva Band===
When the mathematician [[Hao Qinglai]] developed the Quinglai Tables, the Alpha Centauri Flight was nearly ready. The first IEV hull was completed soon afterwards, and the first crew was being trained for the eight year voyage when the [[Dva Band]] of hyperspace was discovered.

This discovery promised to cut the journey from eight years to two years. An [[Orion Spacecraft]] was launched to test both the feasibility of the Quinglai Tables and the usefulness of the [[Dva Band]]. The mission ended in disaster: the Orion was torn apart and all hands were lost.

===Ihorangi Point===
The disaster of the first [[Dva Band]] flight was not enough to curtail the Alpha Centauri Flight. The Space Agency offered a research grant and a significant prize to anyone who could discover a safe way to use the next level of [[hyperspace]].

In [[2019]], Jason Ihorangi was the first to propose that normal space gravity affected [[hyperspace]], and soon proved the existence of what he called the [[Hyperspace Limit]] of a star. He suggested that a ship entering the [[Dva Band]] at a distance of 15AU or greater from [[Sol]] would be safe.

In 2020, an Orion Spacecraft flight made a one light month trip in four days using the [[Dva Band]], proving that it could be safely used at the distances Ihorangi prescribed.

===First Ship of the Class===

On May 7, 2020, the first IEV was completed and ready for its shakedown cruise. It was christened the UEAS ''Pytheas'', after the Greek explorer Pytheas of Massilia. The ''Pytheas'' went on to complete a successful mission, and fourteen additional IEV's were built before the line was retired in favor of larger, faster designs.

==Construction==

An Interstellar Exploration Vehicle is far too large to build on the surface of [[Terra]] and then launch. The first ship of the class was built piece by piece, then launched by [[Orion Spacecraft| Ares IV]] Rockets and assembled at the [[International Space Station]]. By the time the fourth ship in the class was built, the [[Altair Space Station ]] was complete and included a functional shipyard.

===Superstructure===

The first component in the construction of an IEV is the superstructure spine. This 60-meter-long cylindrical metal component was built in six interlocking parts which were welded together in orbit. With a diameter of six meters, it was a massive undertaking. The cylinder was hollow inside, with a single walkway running down the spine with reinforced bulkheads every ten meters. Power and communications wires and potable water pipes run through the spine, secured with insulation.

===Reactor Module===

Next came the reactor module. The reactor was designed along the lines of the former United States Navy ship reactors, taking a similar route with model names and types. The [[Bowman Electric]] Group won the competition for a compact, low-heat fission reactor capable of fitting comfortably in the reactor module. The reactor module was 20 meters long by 30 meters wide, and 15 meters tall, housing the reactor on three decks. The reactor module was connected to the rear of the spine of the ship by a sealed bulkhead, with explosive bolts. In the event of a catastrophe, the reactor could be jettisoned to avoid the loss of the rest of the ship and crew. The Bowman S1B Fission Reactor was rated at 150 megawatts per hour, and operated at only 150<sup>o</sup> Celsius. It has two reaction piles: a water pile for driving turbines to generate electrical power for the ship, and a liquid hydrogen pile for operating the [[Nuclear Thermal Rockets]].

===Engine Module===

Directly behind the reactor module is the engine module, which houses the eight massive [[Nuclear Thermal Rockets]] that drive the IEV, and twenty-four vernier thrusters clustered around the main drives. Unlike a standard water steam reactor, the NTR uses a Plutonium-239 reaction to ignite liquid hydrogen in a controlled burn, giving the ship massive amounts of thrust.

The engine module also houses the Volstamik-Krenschov Jump Drive, which made the whole operation possible.

===Fuel Modules===

The IEV's six fuel modules are located directly above and below the engine module. Each module is a carefully sealed and pressure-regulated liquid hydrogen storage container. The fuel modules can be jettisoned individually or all at once in the event of a disaster. The tanks are self-sealing and can repair minor punctures, and are armored against micrometeor impacts.

===Command Module===

The Command Module is a 25 meter sphere at the front of the spine of the IEV. It is divided into five decks.

The top deck houses the cockpit, astronomy laboratory, survey laboratory, biological laboratory, and two potable water tanks.

The second deck houses the main air scrubbers, a thirty day emergency power supply, emergency food rations, and the zero-g toilet. Crews working on the design often joked at the problem with having a toilet next to the air scrubbers, until it was pointed out that the ship's entire air supply was sealed.

The third deck houses the main sealed bulkhead to the spine, along with the emergency command module release system. In the event of a catastrophe, the third deck had the equipment to sever the command module from the rest of the ship and turn it into an emergency habitat. The third deck also houses an emergency fuel supply for the command module's maneuvering thrusters, and the main computer bank.

The fourth deck houses the main temperature regulation system, along with the water reclamation and hydroponics garden.

The fifth deck houses an observatory and recreation room, which usually included electronic entertainment and workout machines to keep the crew as relaxed and fit as possible during the long voyages.

===Crew Module===

The ten meters of the spine behind the command module houses the crew module, which looks like a second tube slung from the first one. It wraps half-way around the spine, and is eight meters wide, ten meters long, and five meters tall.

It has a single deck, which houses the crew quarters, galley, and the main personal hygiene room with a zero-g shower, sink, and toilet. The crew quarters are slightly cramped, with three bunks to a rack and no privacy. Each crew member has a locker in this area.

===Cargo Modules===

The main cargo module is attached behind the crew module. It is a six meter tall cylinder, four meters in diameter. It houses the food supplies for the crew, mostly freeze-dried or preserved.

The second cargo module is attached behind the first. It is a series of eight pressure-sealed tanks, four on each side of the spine. Each contains potable water, which is fed into the IEV's plumbing system. An electric heater is included in one of the tanks. All eight tanks have input valves from the water reclamation system, which is rated to produce potable water.

===Communications Module===

The center of the spine houses the communications module. It is a ten meter long cylinder, twenty meters in diameter. It houses the main communications room of the ship, as well as the radar systems.

There are massive 4 meter radar dishes on the top and bottom of the communications module, as well as four telemetry antennas and an audio-visual transceiver.

===Docking Module===

Directly between the communications module and the reactor module is the docking module. This module features two cylindrical launch preparation areas on either side of the spine, which can accept the docking collars of Planetary Surface Access Modules or Orion Spacecraft. These preparation areas are four meters high and five meters long. In a normal mission, there are two PSAMs attached, one on each launch preparation area.

The main docking module has spacesuit lockers for the crew, as well as emergency air tanks.

The lower part of the docking module features an extendable docking collar, which can mate to another IEV, to the [[International Space Station]], or to the [[Altair Space Station]]. The main docking collar can extend to up to fifteen meters, but is usually collapsed to only two meters.

===Air Modules===

Because of their long missions, IEVs are equipped with five air modules, each of which contains a year's worth of liquid nitrox. The tanks are each ten meters long, and are housed on the top of the spine except for the area occupied by the communications module. Pressure and heat regulators slowly release just enough air at a time to accomodate the crew's needs.
