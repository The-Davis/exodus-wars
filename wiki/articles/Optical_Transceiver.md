In outer space, most civilizations use optical communications for their combat communications.

Optical communications use pulses of light to transmit binary information. It requires a direct line-of-sight between the transmitter and the receiver, but in space it's difficult to lose direct line-of-sight.

A transmission is recorded, compressed, and transmitted to the receiver, where it is decoded and displayed or broadcast, depending on whether the message is audio or video. The entire process takes microseconds. The computers running the whole process are extremely efficient.

Optical communications cannot be intercepted. The pulse is so short that it is impossible for an enemy to move into line-of-sight, set up receiving equipment, and capture the light pulses.

Optical communication is impossible to jam in outer space. Atmospheric distortions and the easy loss of line-of-sight make it impractical for long-range communications on a planet.
