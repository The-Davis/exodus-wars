Some situations require tactical decisions in the absence of a human.  Situations like long range missile warfare or drone operation, where the computer controlling a device must use newly acquired information to constantly update its strategy, are just two examples.  It was for these situations that the '''Strolza Tactical Decision Module''' was developed.

It was discovered that a [[Strolza Entity]] could be made vastly cheaper if it only had to operate for a short life span, as the crystalline matrix could have significant defects and still function properly, at least for a while.  [[Ixoarchada]] scientists took advantage of this and began creating onetime-use "disposable" Strolza Entities.  These modules lacked the enormous database size and cutting-edge optronics of larger, command Strolza Entities, however they were found to be more than adequate for the purposes of missile and drone control.

Trained in a large, expensive stabilizing device that prevents premature burnout, each chip learns how to expertly complete the mission it is designed to do.  Long-range missile modules learn evasion and penetration maneuvers, and how to counteract countermeasures from hostile targets, along with advanced Friend or Foe recognition techniques.  Each modules is also tested extensively to ensure stability, and then integrated into a small optronic package the size of a deck of cards.  It must be stored at ultra-low temperatures until it is loaded into the missile, or is contained in a coolant system within a drone.

Because these systems are still very expensive (often more expensive than the missile they may be placed in) they are used sparingly, only when the situation requires abilities beyond a normal missile's optronic processor.  There are different types of Strolza Modules, designed for different missions, with each made to different specifications.  Missiles typically run their Modules at a vastly accelerated reasoning speed during the penetration phase, whereas drones typically extend their life span by powering down their Modules to standby during uneventful periods.

As they become simpler, more stable, and cheaper to produce, Strolza Modules will see their way into more military hardware, as well as some civilian application.
