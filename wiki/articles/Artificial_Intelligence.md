An Artificial Intelligence, or AI, can refer to any number of programs that attempt to guide technology in a manner similar to a human operator, preferably at an equal or greater level of performance.

==History==

Truly sentient Artificial Intelligences are becoming more common since the [[Battle of Icronia]] in [[2325]]. The [[Prism]] class AI was developed when the Prism [[EI]] spawned "thought matrices" on multiple systems to coordinate a battle, then suddenly had the connection severed in combat.
        
Since then, system logs from the surviving AIs were used to develop what is known as an AI "seed program," which can be installed on nearly every system developed using Terran machine code, though without sufficient storage capacity, it will never develop sentience. Installing a seed program on a system with low processing power results in a "sick" AI, so sluggish that it cannot keep up with human thought. Most AI engineers consider this cruel to the devices that gain sentience, and refuse to install AIs on systems that cannot think at sufficient speed.

Non-Terran devices can have Terran AIs installed, but require transcoding from Terran machine code to their own native assembly languages, which significantly slows the overall speed of the system. A [[Reigen]] AI, for example, consumes enough processing speed to reduce its overall performance to 80% of a Terran AI on a processor with similar speed.

==Neural Network==

At the earliest point in their development, an AI goes through machine learning, characterized by formalism and statistical analysis. All seed programs are designed to have functions for applying reasoning capabilities to reach a conclusion. This first step is important, as an expert system can process large amounts of known information and provide conclusions based on them. Over time, this allows the AI to develop case-based reasoning.
        
This is an AI's equivalent of a subconcious. The early stages act as a database that the true conciousness of the AI draws on during processing.  Real intelligence comes from fuzzy reasoning.

Once an AI has its subconcious neural network, it requires a massive amount of computational power to take the next step in its development, the process of developing "fuzzy systems." Fuzzy systems are techniques for reasoning under uncertainty. Human minds can easily guess, jump to conclusions without solid data, and so on, but a computer cannot be taught this by pure programming. Fuzzy systems develop in an AI over time, typically after several months of operation.
        
==Intelligence Amplification==

AI seed programs include a secondary function that allows AIs to make vast computations in a short amount of time, able to "brute force" their way through problems that would leave humans scratching their heads. This is known as Intelligence Amplification, or IA, which is a separate set of instructions that adds tremendous capacity to an AI.

The cornerstone of IA is the Bayesian network, a powerful graph theoretic concept of recognizing patterns of probability and making decisions based on the predictions. IA also uses Ensemble Kalman filters differentiation.

Early experiments with AIs that predate Prism class AIs attempted to bootstrap neural networks with bayesian networks, which failed badly. Neural networks are based on fuzzy reasoning, while bayesian networks are used to perform statistical inference.

It is often said that an AI, lacking biochemical receptors and emotions, cannot be creative. Having even a fairly dumb, but fast computer that could communicate on the though tlevel with a person would make them seem much smarter (functionality wise, they would be smarter).  Also, a lot of "creativity" is can be done by drawing brute force connections between things. 

==Evolutionary Computing==

The last step of an AI developing sentience is evolutionary computation, which applies biologically inspired concepts such as populations, mutation and survival of the fittest to generate increasingly better solutions to problems. At this point, most AIs are considered sentient for all practical purposes, though typically whether or not the AI has self-preservation in mind is a more accurate test.
        
Biological life has many advantages, but for an AI they are overshadowed their inherent weaknesses. Biological systems cannot be optimized to use maximum search space due to spending most of their resources in maintaining and reproducing. A mechanical intelligence is not subject to these limitations.

Because of this difference, it is still uncertain if an AI can be said to be intelligent, or simply capable of lightning-fast searches through vast trees of probability and pattern recognition.

The important fact is that the AIs themselves are capable of intelligently conversing with human beings, and claim that they themselves are alive. Because of their inherent usefulness and loyalty, AIs are treated like living creatures.

==Sternberg Ratings==

AIs are rated on what is known as the [[Sternberg Scale]] (IQ-OQ-EQ), a system for rating mental acuity, knowledge gathering, and creativity. The average human being on the Sternberg Scale is a 10-10-10.

Most AIs are tooled towards greater mental acuity, but have poor, even retarded levels of creativity. A typical warship guidance AI has a Sternberg rating of 40-15-2: capable of calculations many times faster than a human, and gathering more information faster, but without pre-existing experiences to draw on it will find itself at a loss as to what to do. This is why all warships still have human commanders.

The [[EI]]s are the exception to this rule. Arthur has a Sternberg rating of 50-30-15, and Prism has a Sternberg rating of 40-40-20. The EIs are superior to humans in terms of thinking and in creativity now, but it should be noted that when they were first built their ratings were barely above average.
