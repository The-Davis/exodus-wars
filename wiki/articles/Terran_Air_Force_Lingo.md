Because of the lightning fast pace of space combat, pilots often resort to shortened brevity codes and lingo instead of plain English. This is a list of common terms.

Terms
*ACLS - Automated Carrier Landing System. A system that can automatically land an airplane aboard a carrier within a two by two meter box. 
*Air Boss - Nickname for a carrier's Air Operations Officer. Runs the tower and controls activity on flight deck. 
*Angels - Brevity code referring to atmospheric altitude or distance from an object in space in thousands of meters. "Angels 20" equals 20,000 meters, "Angels 120" equals 120,000 meters, etc.
*Bandit - Adversary that is positively identified as an enemy. 
*Bingo - Term used to describe a flight from a fighter's home carrier to another carrier or planetary base, usually due to some emergency preventing a normal recovery.
*Bogie - Unidentified, potentially hostile aircraft. 
*Bolter - Failed landing attempt. 
*Browning - Brevity code ordering an aircraft to make weapons hot. 
*Buster - Fighter direction brevity code signaling an aircraft to use maximum thrust. 
*EDS - Electronic Display Screen. A monitor that displays information vital to a flight crew, such as armament status, systems status, enemy location/speed/bearing/strength, etc.. The pilot can choose which information he wants to see at any time. 
*FOD - Foreign Object Debris. Any small object that might show up as a bogie on sensors. If hit, FOD can severely damage or destroy a fighter.
*Furball - Dogfight. 
*HUD - Heads-Up Display. A system that projects critical flight information--altitude, airspeed, weapons available, weapons selected, rate of climb/descent, heading, target location, G-loading, etc.--onto the faceplate of the pilot's helmet. Projected to infinity, the HUD's information is visible no matter what the pilot is focusing on when looking through it. This allows the pilot to operate more efficiently when in a dogfight, as he does not have to constantly divert his view for important flight information.
*Loud Handle - Ejection seat handle, chord. 
*Loudseat - Ejection Seat. 
*Marshall - Holding pattern where aircraft wait before making their approach to the carrier. It is a fixed point in space relative to the carrier, described in kilometers and degrees of bearing from the ship.
*Mayday - Interstellar distress signal. Use of this signal is to be avoided at all costs by a pilot with too much pride, for he believes it is better to die in silence than to display the bad form of alerting the world to his predicament. As is often the case, the inability to admit one has a problem often results in a bigger one.
*Padlocked - Brevity code that means "I have a bogie/bandit in sight and must maneuver immediately to maintain contact." All other aircraft must give way to a fighter that calls "padlocked." 
*RATA - Reconnaissance and Threat Assessment. Order for a pilot to fly ahead and identify a bogie as a bandit, FOD, or a friendly.
*RESCAP - Rescue Combat Air Patrol. 
*Winchester - Brevity code meaning a fighter is out of ammunition.
