Using helicopters to move infantry around the battlefield is not a new idea. Since the mid 20th century, armies have seen the value of the helicopter's mobility and flexibility. The late 21st century's [[First Exodus War]] introduced the concept of dropping troops in from orbit. The invading [[Torimur]] rebels failed to gain much ground, but it was a novel idea and foreshadowed the development of the dropship.

Moving Infantry around is always a touchy matter. Dropships are best for dropping from orbit, but once deployed on a planet, troops are usually in for the long haul. Moving across terrain at high speed usually means taking an APC--which tended to be slow and armored--or a helicopter--which tended to be fast but vulnerable. The rotors in a helicopter and delicate internal systems were simply too delicate and vulnerable to anti-aircraft fire.

In an attempt to correct these flaws, the Royal Imperial Army borrowed several developments from the Royal Imperial Air Force, and began work on the XH-1, the first hybridized VTOL that could work without the use of rotor blades. It used a variable-thrust Ramjet with afterburners, coupled with a particle shield to act as an invisible wing. While it was equipped with an ion propulsion system for space maneuvering and thrust, it could not carry enough reaction mass for significant space operations. The XH-2 refined the particle field generator to a point where it was light enough for a single person to carry. With the mass freed up, the XH-2 could achieve escape velocity and fly to a high stable orbit, then dock with a ship or station in orbit.

The XH-3 was the first VTOL-dropship designed to carry troops to and from the surface at high speeds, and operate on the surface. Its ramjets had an unlimited power supply from a fission reactor, and it had enough reaction mass for two hours of sustained thrust in space from its ion propulsion system. The YH-3 was tested extensively, but the particle field generators failed in combat testing, costing several crews their lives. Instead, traditional wings were installed for atmospheric operations, which could be retracted in space to allow it to fit on carrier decks and cruiser welldecks. The design was finally finished, and designated the UH-3 Skyhawk.

The UH-3 went through several variants before it reached its current design. Its ion propulsion system was replaced with a magnetoplasmadynamic thruster, creating the UH-3A. An aeroelastic control system was added to the UH-3B, and [[Rahn Industries]] began heavy production of the design for the [[Prefecture]]. All [[Rahn Industries]]-built Skyhawks received the "r" sub-designation. Rahn Industries introduced the addition of [[Ixoarchada]] anti-gravity technology into the UH-3Cr, and the final and current generation of the Skyhawk, the UH-3D, uses gravity cavitation drive systems.

The UH-3D is one of the most produced and used VTOL designs in the galaxy. The original production company, Lycoming Airworks, still builds the UH-3D on Alpha Centauri for the Colonial Commonwealth. Rahn Industries builds the UH-3Dr under license for the Federated Districts of the Prefecture. Sigori Helicopters, based on Terra, builds the UH-3Ds for the Sol Defense Force. Mesarbauer, the premier Mesarthrim aerospace design firm, has a slightly improved version they build for the Luftwaffe, designated the UH-3Dm Luftfalke, though some believe the UH-3Dm deserves an "E" variant or an entirely different design specification. Only the airframe and general operating characteristics are the same.

The Skyhawk has changed over the years, and many argue that some of the changes have been for the worse. Early Skyhawk variants could operate as "gunships", leaving their cargo space empty in favor of massive cannon and missile loads. The UH-3D had its fire control systems removed, but Rahn Industries plans to add them back in to the UH-3Er variant and re-release the gunship. The A and B variants were armed with machine gun turrets, but they have been removed due to poor targetting and fire arcs.

The modern Skyhawk is much more capable than the original. While still a VTOL, the Skyhawk is now also a functional aerospace vehicle, capable of flight in atmosphere and space without its antigravity lift system. When coupled with its lift, the Skyhawk is actually an acrobatic ship, capable of maneuvers that some fighters would not try. It can function as a VTOL with its wings folded, and can function as both with its wings deployed.

Normally, the Skyhawk carries fourteen infantry. The infantry can deploy almost instantly, under cover of Lightning VTOL gunships. The Skyhawk used to be capable of outstanding solo missions, and is planned to be re-equipped with its cannons and flexible loads soon. For additional ammunition and weapons such as Field Guns, the Skyhawk can carry two light Cargo Pods, which it can deploy with a simple push of a button from the pilot.

The Skyhawk remains the single most popular VTOL design around. It is used by civilian corporations and by five major militaries. The Skyhawk has proven itself an effective troop transport and combat VTOL, and has been in most major engagements since the invasion of Terra in 2315. It proved itself particularly capable against the Volucris when it was used extensively for infantry operations in the Battle of Icronia.
