by Austin Bailey

=====The Lorithan Wastes=====

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A soldier surveys the bleak scene about him, standing in the ruins of a watch tower.  The bridge before him fades into the distance, the opposite side barely visible through the murky haze perpetually blighting the landscape.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;''Thirty clicks to the [[Korval Line]]…another twenty to the maglifts.  Ten minutes.''  A quick thought.  ''Too long''.  A query to his interface—he’d have three.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A quick glance down at his charge reassures him.  A child’s exhausted frame lies huddled on the ground at his feet. The soldier thinks back to how he found her, crying beside the bodies of her parents, and he shudders. ''Any price''.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;One minute ticks by, though adrenaline and neuraccel residual make it pass a bit more slowly.  The child pulls closer, gripping his armor-clad leg tightly.  She’s ugly by most standards, scarred from birth by this wretched waste the natives call a homeworld—but she’s the very face of [[Halivin]] for his weary eyes.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“Fehr tu Kihtor, Kida?” she murmurs.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
“Yes.  Not much longer, now,” he replies.  She doesn’t register the double meaning.
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;His rifle drops to the ground—he has no more need of it.
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“Fiv ka Sasha,” and he picks her up, holding her against his chest.  He puts on his helmet as she gasps, again amazed as they rise from the ground.  A tear in her eyes, a prayer on his lips, and they’re off.  
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The monsters are waiting, their numbers beyond count. Like a bolt from heaven, the soldier and the girl fly through them. Sensors overwhelmed, he looks into the IR and UV spectrums for better vision.  The vanguard of the swarm is before him, the body of the beast at his back.  But the Fates are with him, and they guide him on.
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;He reaches the vanguard, and his foes turn to face him.  No battle can be made, not here, not with his precious cargo.  Shields on, drives at peak, he weaves through the ravening tide.  But the bulk is still to come, and his foes grow thicker, their blades and barbs, no harm to him, threaten a young girl’s life.  Plasma and heat fill the air around him, a storm of single-minded death directed to halt him.  Countless thousands and tens of thousands work against them—only a soldier and a child.
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;''Now.''  An unspoken command.  Chemicals release, nanites disperse, and optronic accelerators activate in his brain.  Time slows, nearly stalls, but continues on.  She doesn’t notice, but his world, indeed his final moments, are set now in slow-motion. 
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Over days, he’s used the process in bursts, saving her life, but it has taken its toll, and the suit’s interface lets him know.  Warnings flash a countdown timer, but he ignores it.  He is running his last race, and a final fatal slalom through this forest of death requires all of his focus.  
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In his eyes, his enemies’ movements are slow and cumbersome, and he adjusts to their every attack to shelter his consignment.  He dodges and swerves, twists and dives, every ounce of his being focused on his destination.  
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The bridge ends, and the land before him opens out onto the plateau called Rikaz Jael.  More foes, more strikes, he dodges what he can.  Blow after blow evaded, but some find their mark—there are too many.
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Two minutes left, his interface pings.  Ten clicks to the Korval Line.  He sees the position in the distance.  
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;''Too many enemies!  I need more speed.''  Holding the girl tighter, both arms now envelope her small body, and he hunches over her a little more.  Shields drop, and the drives charge on faster, every ounce of the suits remaining power now thrusting it ever faster, faster, towards a little girl’s only escape from this nightmare.  ''Protect us, fathers.  Protect her,'' he prays.
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The drives protest, systems announce their strain, and his interface registers alarms.  A little girl’s cry vetoes them all, and a soldier charges forward.  ''Only a minute left!  I have to make it.''
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Emerging from the horde, the frenzied sea of rage, they approach the hills of Rikaz Jael.  ''Fates!  The Aetoni—-they’re still here!''  He streaks towards their position, no time to stop, to help.  A salute is registered, cover fire laid, and he hastens past doomed men screaming their defiance, fighting to their last breath as [[Praviil]] comes to claim them. No time to look back, his enemy is close behind, but he knows well the scene.  ''Brave fools.''
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A silent claxon sounds in his mind, but there is not a moment to spare.  He runs through protocols, disabling the final safeguards on the neural accelerators.  ''Must have more time.''  He hurriedly programs final operations into his armor, activating various single-use protocols, and sending a coded burst-transmission to the Kalidasans at the maglift station. 
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The accelerators want to shut-down, but he’s removed the failsafes and they continue on, the amount of neuraccel in his brain now lethal.  Nanites scramble to repair rapidly decaying neural pathways—a futile act.  Were he to stop now, still his fate would be sealed.  He’s spent too long in this state over the past few days, his mind now beyond repair.  The suit is finally programmed, the enemy far behind.  ''Bless the Aetoni''.
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;At last, the accelerators overload, give-out, and a bright warning displays in his ocular implants.  ''Only a few seconds now.''  He looks down at a little girl looking up at him.
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“We’re close now?” she asks.  
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“Very close now, Sasha.  Almost home…”
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;His voice trails off; his vision blurs.  
Finally in Illinaul, “Guide me Halivin, on the path of my father.”
''Goodbye Sasha.''
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Kalidasan soldiers dash from a bunker and grab a small girl from the arms of an [[Ansau]] suit, rushing to take her to the last maglift at the station.  She turns back, looking at the suit, reaching out.  
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“Aren’t you coming?  We’re here.  Let’s go home!  Kida!” 
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;She gets no response as she’s rushed onto the train.  Sasha darts to the window, crying, unable to speak.  The maglift rises from the ground and accelerates quickly, as outside her window a lifeless suit stares blankly back at her.  In seconds, it is beyond sight, fading into the distance as the maglift climbs the [[Kihtor]].
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Kalidasan soldiers at the station head for their bunkers, paying silent homage to the now lifeless carapace, still barely powered by a dying energy cell.  Blood drains from several joints, whole armor sections are broken and missing, and a dead soldier lies silently interred in this most fitting of coffins.  
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Kalidasan guns are silenced barely a half hour later, and a little girl stares at the carnage from the shelter of the mighty Kihtor.
<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“We’re home now, Kida.  We’re home.”
