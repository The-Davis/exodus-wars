'''Ion Propulsion''' is a form of electric propulsion. It uses beams of ions — electrically charged atoms or molecules — for propulsion. The precise method for accelerating the ions may vary, but all designs take advantage of the charge-to-mass ratio of ions to accelerate them to very high velocities using a high electric field. Ion thrusters are able to achieve high specific impulse, reducing the amount of reaction mass required, but increasing the amount of power required compared to chemical rockets. Ion thrusters can deliver one order of magnitude greater propellant efficiency than traditional liquid fuel rocket engines.

The most common type of ion drive was the ''Field Emission Electric Propulsion'', an advanced electrostatic propulsion concept that used liquid cesium as a propellant. It consisted of an emitter and an accelerator electrode. If given enough power, reaction mass and emitters, the "FEEP" could accelerate a spacecraft at up to 100KPH/S.
