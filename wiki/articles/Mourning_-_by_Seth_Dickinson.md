==== The Jerome Black, First Fleet Flagship ====
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The pneumatic hiss of the opening hatch blends into Admiral Robert Marshall’s yawn. Blinking wearily, he tosses his kit bag into his quarters and staggers after it.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;He is greeted by barren walls and spartan furnishing, all gunmetal-gray and lifeless; but the lighting is warm, and soft music pipes through the room’s intercom. Marshall frowns as he hears the tune; a pang of nostalgia pins him to the spot. I haven’t heard that since before the first war...<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The melody is an ancient hero’s lament for fallen comrades, and it has never failed to break his warrior’s trance and summon vivid emotion. Marshall sags into a chair, feeling the weight of years pressing in his shoulders. He squeezes his eyelids shut, and images blossom in the darkness. Blurred faces parade past his mind’s eye. Some are dear friends, some are men and women he barely knew, and some…some are family.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;All of them are dead. He has watched them devoured by fire, chewed apart by a hundred kinds of weapons, asphyxiated or frozen in the heartless depths of space, or fallen to sickness and plague.  All of the deaths are his responsibility…all of them perished under his command.  He can see the specters saluting as they fade.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;One face remains vivid, each millimeter etched with perfect detail on the surface of his brain, scarred there forever by the searing brand of loss, for nothing is more painful than losing a child…and nothing is more memorable than pain.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Jordan…<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Admiral opens his eyes and unconsciously stiffens into a parade-rest stance. A single, diamond tear slides down his face; then the flow is staunched and he banishes the image of his fallen son back into the reaches of memory.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;He raises his face and addresses the ceiling. “Prism? Where’d you find the tune?”<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“I…thought it might be appropriate, sir,” Prism replies. The EI’s voice is uncharacteristically quiet, almost tremulous.  “There’s a transmission for you, sir.”<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Marshall almost breaks.  He knows what it will be, but he fights down the pain with the stoic strength of the human race’s oldest warrior. His voice cracks. “S…send it through, Prism.”<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“I’m sorry, sir,” the EI murmurs, as the familiar, ghoulish words--words he himself has written far too many times--spring into life on the chair’s display.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“We regret to inform you that your adopted son, Jeff Nolan…”<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Marshall lets the silent tears flow, washing away his pain.<br>

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;An eternity later – or is it only minutes?- he stares sightlessly into the void. The windows of the ''Jerome Black''’s observation deck are clear as Torimur’s air all those faint-remembered years – centuries? - ago. He remembers that fateful morning as if it was yesterday, as clearly as he remembers Jordan’s face and the rich timbre of Jeff’s laugh.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;He drank in the horror, unable to look away but agonized by what he saw, torn between horror and fury. He saw the atmosphere boiling away…light blazing through the superheated air in twisting auroras as the warheads exploded. The crust blackened and fractured under the hammer of thermonuclear detonations; magma welled to the surface, as the world itself is bled to death.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A soft moan, like a death rattle, escaped from him as another blaze of nuclear fury erupted near Cairn Peak, his home. He watched the lakes boiled away into a cloud of steam; then the mountain itself vanished, leaving only a blackened crater, a sore of oozing, molten rock.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;''There’s no one left.  They’re all…dead.''<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;''Dead like Jeff and Jordan and everyone I ever loved…father, mother, brothers, sisters.  Maya, my lovely bride.  Who’s left who remembers the pain? Hadrian remembers.  And Lucius Black, damn him.  We’re dying, one by one, and soon there won’t be anyone left who remembers.''<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;''Is that such a bad thing''?<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“Sir!” Prism’s shout brings him back to reality. His view of space is blurred. ''Did we make a hyperspace jump''?<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Then his brain recognizes the low sobbing noise. ''Crying again? I’m getting soft in my old age…damn. Damn.''<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;One of his hands is splayed, claw-like, on the viewport, reaching out into the void in a futile effort to reclaim the precious treasure he’s lost.  His thumbnail has sliced through his skin, and when he draws his hand back from the window, a crimson handprint stains the pristine surface. Blood is on my hands.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“Sorry, Prism. I’m here. I’m…here.”<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“Sir,” the EI says, sounding tentative. Suddenly, the words spill out of her in a rush, as if she’s unaccustomed to being anything more than a processor of orders, the ultimate military mind. “There was nothing you could have done to save him. He was a soldier. He knew the risks. He died doing his duty.”<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“Any more clichés for me, Prism?” Marshall snaps, feeling sudden anger. “Any more blasé comfort to offer an old man with no children?”<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is a long silence.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;“Only that…he won’t be forgotten,” Prism finally murmurs. “None of you will be forgotten. I promise.”<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Marshall sighs, nodding.  For a moment, the silence of space pours into the observation deck.  The peace is only enjoyed by Prism and Marshall, two ancient beings in a changed universe, sharing a moment of camaraderie in the face of an uncertain future.
