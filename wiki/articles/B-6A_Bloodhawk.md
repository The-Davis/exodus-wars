The Prefecture took over the design line at the height of the Battle of Terra, and captured the new heavy XF-13 airframe. It was ideal for a strategic bomber and torpedo delivery system, as both the Empire and the Prefecture had begun using shield technologies on their warships that the nuclear weapons of the F-9B could not penetrate. The XB-6 was armed with heavy torpedoes, internal bomb bays, and an enormous array of ordinance. The YB-6 prototype fought in the Battle of Canaveral and the Siege of Orlando, and there were B-6 Bloodhawks in use by the final Assault on McKinley.

The Bloodhawk was initially built in early 2323 to create a heavy assault bomber. The Stuka was a good attack weapon, but the Bloodhawk took over heavy assault entirely. With its crew to handle to myriad of operations and huge payload, the Bloodhawk could simply handle more assault missions than a Stuka. Sky Marshal Jan Brooks ordered an overhaul of the Bloodhawk in June of 2324. Albany Aerowerks engineers were given a difficult task: fitting an aerospace bomber craft with a gravity shield generator.

Because of its size, the Bloodhawk has two pieces of equipment that no other aerospace model can carry. A simple 1xC FTL drive to carry it from planet to planet in a starsystem, and a gravity shield. Even with four fusion reactors, the Bloodhawk cannot maintain speed and power the shield at the same time. Instead, the shield feeds off a battery, and can stay up for a minute at a time. While the shield is off, the reactors slowly charge the battery. It takes ten minutes to recharge. Still, the gravity shield is an awesome addition to the already formidable defenses of the Bloodhawk.

The Bloodhawk's pilot is located in the foremost cockpit. He handles the piloting of the bomber, and monitors reactor output and shield levels. With all the ammunition fired and ordinance dropped in the course of battle, the Bloodhawk's mass and acceleration abilities change many times. Even with the powerful on board computers, the calculations and corrections necessary are a full-time job.

Just above and behind the pilot is the navigator of the Bloodhawk. He designates targets to the bombardier and sets the defensive firing patterns of the anti-missile system. The four Large Neutron Cannons at the front of the Bloodhawk are under his control, and he can take command of any station--gunners, pilot, or bombardier--in an emergency.

The bulbous top cockpit of the Bloodhawk houses the bombardier. He controls the bomb-bay in the belly of the Bloodhawk, the ordinance on the four hard-points, and the fixed wing-mounted cannons and missile launchers. He also has input from the Target Acquisition Gear and other sensors.

To give the bombardier time to acquire a proper target and fire accurately, the Bloodhawk's designers equipped it with three defensive turrets and cockpits for gunners. Two turrets are controlled by the gunners, and the third is controlled by a tracking AI. The captain/navigator or the AI can switch the control assignments when necessary. The AI has a subroutine to aid the gunners in aiming. In most firing tests, an AI/human team is more accurate than an AI or human alone.

The standard payload on the hard-points of the Bloodhawk is for heavy warship or ground assault. The tail hard-points carry four LRM launchers, which it uses to distract anti-missile systems. Once the LRMs are launched, it releases its Orca torpedoes. Orcas are popular in warship-to-warship combat, and can do serious damage even to a shielded vessel. Against an unshielded vessel, it can crack the hull.

Overhauling the Bloodhawk only took a month. By July of 2324, every bomber was completely re-equipped and back in the field. It is still deployed conservatively, as it is expensive to maintain and repair, but one or two Bloodhawks is more than enough to succeed in a heavy assault. Besides fleet and ground garrison deployment, the Bloodhawk is often sent on long-range assault and patrol missions, as it needs no carrier support to travel throughout a starsystem, and can use Wormhole Gates to get from starsystem to starsystem.
