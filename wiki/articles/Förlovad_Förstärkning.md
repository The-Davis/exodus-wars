Set after "Tempest: Diplomacy by Other Means"

by Jamal Coleman

=====Merchant Fleet Central Logistics Office - Icronia High Orbit - 1245 Hours=====
     Kommodör Diamant Hammare rises from his chair to meet Admiral Robert Marshall, who is escorted into the Kommodör’s office by an assistant. 
     “Welcome, Admiral Marshall.” He extends his arm in greeting, and the men clasp at the wrist in Icronian fashion. Hammare turns to his assistant. “That will be all, Märin.” 
     His assistant closes the door behind her as she leaves. Hammare gestures irritably at the paperwork and PDAs cluttering his office. “Please have a seat and forgive the mess. I’m sure you’ve already realized that the Consortium is going through some major changes” 
     “Indeed. And I understand that congratulations are in order.” Marshall nods at the new rank pins on Hammare’s uniform. 
     “Thank you, though I’m not ready to celebrate just yet,” Hammare sits heavily in his own seat. 
     Marshall shudders slightly from the cold temperature. They both know that enough pleasantries have been exchanged, and are ready to get down to business. 
     “Your Gate in the Leskovik system was destroyed by the same UCS force that nuked our planet.” Marshall explains, tossing a data chip from his vest onto Hammare’s desk. 
     Hammare takes the chip and inserts it into a nearby PDA. He shakes his head slowly, whistling softly at the estimates on the strength of the hostile Mesarthrim fleet. 
     “Just how accurate are these numbers?” 
     “Norman Byar gave them to me personally,” Marshall grunts. “The old weasel wouldn’t even trust his own Inquisitors with the task. I’ll vouch for it.” 
     “These numbers surpass our worst-case scenarios!” Hammare exclaims. “Where could they have come up with these numbers? Just how long have they been planning to attack you?” 
     “I don’t know,” Marshall admits. “We’re prepared to face these…barbaric attacks on our own, but…” he sighs, his steely gray eyes boring into Hammare. “With the destruction of a Gate, the death of its crew, and the very clear and present threat to Consortium assets...” A sly grin split’s the old fox’s face. 
     “Say no more, Admiral,” Hammare smiles. “Say no more.” Hammare punches a few buttons on his keyboard and a holographic image appears. “The Consortium stands by its allies, particularly the Prefecture after what you did for us. The shareholders have approved the assignment of the newly formed Ingripandekraften—that would be Icronian Intervention Force in your language—to assist your government.” 
     The display scrolls down a long list of numbers, indicating the classes, numbers, and fighter complements of every ship of the Ingripandekraften. Marshall’s eyebrows rise slightly, and he squints at the list. 
     “Hammare, this looks like your entire fleet.” 
     “Don’t act so surprised, Admiral. We do control more manufacturing capacity than the rest of the Alliance combined,” Hammare grins. “Despite the size of the Ingripandekraften, our homeworld will remain very well protected.” Hammare shuts down the hologram and hands a new data chip to Marshall. “This information should be adequate for your subordinates to help plan the defense of your worlds.” 
     Marshall nods. I’ll take businessmen over bureaucrats any day, he muses. “Our current plan is to deploy your forces defensively in our territory,” he explains. “We want you to defend your Gates and our shipping lanes, leaving my ships free to directly strike at the United Centusi States.” If I had my way, their whole fleet would be on the front. Their ships are better than ours. If they’d just openly declare war on the Mesarthrim, we could end this so quickly. 
     Hammare sighs. He, too, would rather have his ships and their eager crews on the front line; but politics dictated otherwise. Tugging at his beard, he continues. “I will be coordinating logistics with Prefecture Naval Command on deployment of Icronian forces from the Gulrýnnur. The Ingripandekraften will be ready to deploy in three days, the Grindvaktare stations are already on the way to the most strategic Gates, but most won’t be there for several days.” 
     The two men stand and once again shake hands, this time in Terran fashion. “I look forward to serving with you and your men, Diamant.” 
     “I look forward to serving you and yours,” Diamant Hammare smiles. “I hope that this war will only be the first of many joint ventures between our people.” 
     “Agreed,” Marshall nods. Like joint ventures against the Volucris, the real enemy. Hammare was shot down over Oselo; he knows the true threat all too well. It’s a shame the Mesarthrim don’t share his wisdom. 
     Hammare opens his office door. Two Prefecture Marines, an honor guard, are standing at attention, and immediately salute Admiral Marshall. He returns the salute and they escort him to the transport hanger. 
     Hammare turns to his assistant. “Märin, I want you to set up a meeting with Kommodörs Breyskja and Lax of the Gulrýnnur. Make it three hours from now, on their ship. 
     “Yes, sir,” she complies. 
     As the assistant turns to her computer, Hammare sags back into his chair, thinking of the fighting still to come. He flexes his left hand, feeling the slightly unnatural texture of new-grown flesh. For a few seconds he is back in the cockpit of his burning fighter, falling through a storm of Shivas towards the tundra kilometers below. 
     The mental image of burning Oselo still haunts him, even after the rest of the flashback fades. I will not let that happen anywhere else, Hammare resolves. It doesn’t matter who starts it. Volucris or Mesarthrim beware: the Icronians are warriors again.
