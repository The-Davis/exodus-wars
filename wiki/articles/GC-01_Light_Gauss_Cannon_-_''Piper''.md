The Gauss Cannon is a medium energy form of the Mass Driver, capable of launching projectiles at high velocities.

While Autocannons are extremely powerful weapons, there was a serious problem with using them in spaceborne weaponry. The [[Onyx Empire]]'s [[Royal Imperial Air Force]] and [[Royal Imperial Navy]] shared the same weapons development programs in the early years of the [[24th Century]].

The "Piper" was the first Gauss Cannon design developed by the [[Royal Imperial Labs]]. It was designated GC-01, and was developed to be used as a main weapon for the [[Royal Imperial Air Force|Air Force]]'s [[:Category:Fighters|fighters]], and as a the first "Close-In Weapon System" for the [[Royal Imperial Navy|Navy]]'s [[:Category:Warships|warships]].

Instead of using explosive solid propellants to lob ammunition, the GC-01 used a powerful magnetic accelerator to hurl shells of nickel-ferrous alloy at its target. The term "railgun" is actually synonymous with Gauss Cannon.

The "Piper" derived its name from the signature high-pitched whine of the capacitor it used to charge up the magnetic accelerator. When fired at a high rate, the whine sounded almost like a fife or similar pipe being played at a single, constant note.

The Piper was prone to several issues early on. Its capacitors burned out easily: they simply couldn't handle the stress of constant draining and charging the extreme amounts of energy needed to fire the weapon. Additionally, the magnetic coils tended to snap under the stress of quickly changing temperatures.

Despite the issues the GC-1 suffered, the Piper was very popular and was usually jury-rigged in the field by work crews. The [[Royal Imperial Air Force]] and [[Royal Imperial Navy]] used the design from [[2300]] to [[2316]].
