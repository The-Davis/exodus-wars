Railgun and Coilgun The Coilgun is one of the mainstay weapons used in Meronir's Republic, and traces its roots to the dawn of the Meroniri Revolutionary Age (5000-4500BC). The Meroniri people are legendary for their fascination with magnets and their expertise in the manipulation of magnetic fields. Their post-Sundering industry began with the magnet, and their modern weaponry began with the discovery of the electricity and the electromagnet. The Railgun - One of the earliest post-Sundering Meroniri powered weapons was the Railgun. Unlike human civilization, explosives were not harnessed as weapons first. The early Railgun was similar in size and portability to a Terran 16th Century field artillery piece. The hydroelectric-powered machine was a beast to handle, but was a first step towards the modern Coilgun. The Railgun worked by magnetizing a series of metal plates, and placing an oppositely-poled slug in the space between the plates. The floating slug was then accelerated to near-sonic speeds by changing the magnetism of the plates in sequence until the slug exited the barrel of the weapon. The Revolutionary Age Railgun was plagued with problems. Very frequently, the slug would get between the plates and get wedged, or lose its reverse magnetism and bond with the barrel, or not build up enough speed and simply fall out of the barrel. By the end of the Revolutionary Age, the Railgun was a refined weapon, but never got smaller than a cannon. Meroniri didn't develop handheld guns until they discovered gunpowder. The Coilgun - The Coilgun was invented in the Meroniri Atomic Age (2650BC). The Coilgun is simply a Railgun, but on a much smaller scale. Instead of magnetized metal plates, Meroniri scientists discovered how to give individual molecules a magnetic charge far beyond their normal capacity. They refined this to the point where a simple Railgun could be built with only a few iron molecules--quite a scientific achievement. Unfortunately, creating a barrel long enough to pick up killing speed led to lengths just as great as before. A ten meter long, ultra-narrow gun was absurd. Setting up a gentle curve in the barrel to conserve space was a simple matter. The more circles, the longer the barrel. This tightly-coiled barrel earned the Coilgun its name. With "bullets" the size of a single biological cell and miles of barrel length in a tiny coil, the magnetic gun became commonplace in every aspect of arsenal, from tiny covert guns to massive warship cannons. The tiny size of the bullet is necessary to keep the massive amount of energy from destroying the gun and user from simple recoil. Modern handheld Coilguns are housed in a pistol or rifle stock. The stock holds the power cell necessary to charge the magnets, and also holds the block of iron which is fed into the barrel in cell-sized bullets. The number of shots a Coilgun can fire is determined by the amount of energy in its power cell, as a cubic centimeter of iron is enough to keep the weapon armed for a long time. Vehicle and warship Coilguns are much larger than handheld weapons, and the iron bullet-cells they fire reach near-relativistic speeds. The gun casing must be much larger, but the power comes from the near infinite energy of the nuclear fusion reactor of the vehicle, Rig, fighter or warship.
