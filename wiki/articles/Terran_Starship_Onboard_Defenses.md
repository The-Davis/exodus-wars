==Overview==

Since the [[Battle of Terra]], many changes have been made to the way internal security of [[Terran]] warships are handled.  Some ships changed sides several times in the course of the battle, due to inadequate onboard defenses.

Today, warship defenses are state-of-the-art, and almost impossible to take over.  Smaller frigates are limited in their defensive capabilities, while flagships like the Jerome Black are veritable fortresses.

==Defensive Personnel==

First and foremost, the warship relies on its complement of Marines.  On small ships, such as a corvette, this can be as few as five.  On flagships they can number in the thousands.  Shipboard troops have main-battle rifles, grenades, squad automatic weapons, field guns, trip-mines, battle armor, and sometimes heavy ordinance like LAAWs.

Backing up the troops is the ship's crew.  Sometimes, this is a small number, but can also number in the thousands.  Navy personnel are given standard issue sidearms, and low-profile armor vests.  In a pinch, this crew can pick up spare backup gear from the shipboard troops.

==Breaching the Hull==

On some vessels, breaching the hull is relatively simple.  Hangars and flight decks are in place on carriers, allowing easy access for dropships.  On others, there are only docking collars, which are well defended.  If the boarders wish to have some surprise on their side, they must cut their own entrance. This would give the defenders some time to set up defenses, but not as much as if they landed on a collar or went through a hangar.

==Self Destruct Mechanisms==

Assuming they overwhelm the defenders, the boarders have to deal with the attempted self-destruct of the ship. There are multiple self-destruct systems in place.

===Command Station===

First, there is the command station's self-destruct system. This destabilizes the reactor, starts the countdown for self-destruct charges, and sets all remaining ship ammunition to explode. It also destabilizes the cyclotrons of the neutron cannons, irradiating the ship.  Starting this process requires the ship's commanding officer to give a two-second vocal command.  There is an default fifteen minute countdown to allow for evacuation, but the commanding officer can tell the ship exactly how much of a countdown to give.  Destruction is total, and the ship's remains are atomized by the blast.

Stopping this process can be done by a three-step counter-command (requiring the commanding officer's DNA, voiceprint, and passcode). Failing that, each system must be stabilized manually.

This leads us to the decentralized command structure of flagships.  On most warships, the bridge is the heart and mind of the vessel.  On a flagship, the bridge, while vital to the combat effectiveness of the vessel, is not the only command and control system.  There is an engine room, a shield room, and multiple weapon control stations.  Each of these must be independently seized, pacified, and controlled to take over the ship.  Each of these stations has its own self-destruct system.

===Engine Room===

The engine room can start the reactor destabilization. This can be shut down by the chief engineer, or any three subordinates at once, or by the commanding officer.  It must be done by the commanding officer from the bridge, or by the engineers in the engine room.  Like the command system, it requires DNA, voiceprints, and passcodes.

===Shield Room===

The shield room has access to 1/10th of the destructive charges. It can detonate enough to break up the ship, but not enough to atomize it.  It can be shut down by the defense officer in the shield room, or by the commanding officer from the bridge.  As always, DNA, voiceprints and passcodes are needed.

===Weapon Control Stations===

Each weapon control station can cook off enough ammunition to gut the ship from the inside, but not enough to break it up or atomize it.  Each one can also irradiate the ship. They can be shut down at any weapon control station, or from the bridge. Since this self-destruct method can be started up so easily, it only requires a passcode and a DNA source on-file. Any crewmember can shut it down.

==Canceling Self Destruct==

All self-destruct mechanisms can be stopped by jettisoning the reactor core, jettisoning the weapon ammunition, venting the deuteron that powers the Neutron Cannons, and shutting down the main computer to stop the self-destruct charges.  If this is done, it takes four months to bring a ship back up to combat readiness, given that each ship's computer is purpose-built for that particular ship, and has its own special classified security systems.

A ship saved by this method would be effectively blind, immobile, unshielded, and weaponless.  Backup power systems could sustain life support and maneuvering thrusters.  If KF drives are charged, it could make one jump.

This mechanism can be triggered by any crew member of Third Class NCO ranking or higher, unless the self-destruct system was triggered by the commanding officer.  In the event that the commanding officer has issued the self-destruct order, manual overrides must be used to save the ship.  These are distributed in the superstructure of the vessel, in classified locations.  Any officer of Lieutenant Commander rank or higher would know the location of the manual overrides.

==Taking Control==

After the crew is pacified or killed, and the self-destruct systems dealt with without rendering the ship useless, turning the captured vessel into a weapon for the boarding team must be done.

The ship's computer controls roughly 60% of the activity aboard.  Each computer is designed in total secrecy, and is purpose built for each ship.  No two computers are the same.  Suborning the computer is difficult, but not impossible. It would take several years to brute-force decrypt the codes necessary to do so, unless the boarding team was very, very lucky.  It would be best to simply "kill" the computer by shooting out the core--this would also render the self-destruct charges useless, though the reactor self-destruct and the weapons self-destruct would still be operable.

If the computer is suborned, the boarders must then physically take over the control stations, which use simpler independent computer systems.  These independent systems each have their own encryption and passcode systems.  To login, the potential user must give a DNA sample and pass voiceprint identification.  After three failed attempts, the control station wipes itself and is useless.  Backup systems are modular and easy to install, but must be identical in specifications to the original.  It takes five minutes to swap out the wiped components of a control station.

During normal successful logins, after more than twenty seconds of inactivity, the computer logs itself out.  It only takes one second for an authorized person to login, so this time limit is not a problem within normal operating parameters.

==Summary==
Given the odds, running a successful takeover operation is almost impossible. It would take a lot of planning, preparation, and downright luck. The commanding officer has the kill-everyone switch, so that makes things very hairy. Someone would have to figure out how to take him down before he could start the self-destruct.
