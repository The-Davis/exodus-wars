The concept of cryogenic hibernation has fascinated people for years. It was originally envisioned as a means of keeping patients alive until treatments could be discovered for their diseases.

Working [[Terran]] cryogenics that didn't kill the patient were first built in [[1998]]. Individual organs of rats were frozen and brought back without damage. It wasn't until [[2016]] that cryogenic stasis for humans was invented. The first cryo-stasis chamber was huge.

It was developed on [[Luna]] for the purpose of keeping colonists bound for the newly-discovered planets quiet and out of the way of the ship crews who were transporting them. These relatively short naps were usually only five or six years in length.

Cryogenics got its first real test was in the year [[2100]]. The two-hundred year long trip across the galaxy known as the [[Second Exodus]] was traveled by thousands of [[Torimur]] colonials frozen in stasis. At the end of the journey, the first problem with cryogenics--or "Cold Sleep" as most people called it--was discovered.

Most cryogenic engineers believed that the entire person was completely frozen. All physical evidence supported this theory. When 20% of the [[Torimur]] colonists awoke afflicted with varying degrees of insanity, it became apparent that something was very wrong with the popular theory. 

Scientists have been unable to find anything wrong with the freezing systems. As far as they can tell, the entire body is instantly and evenly frozen, and likewise instantly and evenly thawed. No areas of the brain remain unfrozen or frozen longer than any other part of the body, so no problem should occur. Science cannot answer the question.

The popular non-scientific theory is that the human being has a meta-physical part, the mind or soul, which cannot be affected by the freezing process. In long periods of freezing, some minds break and end up suffering from insanity. It is interesting to note that some animals can be affected by the cold sleep insanity that humans suffer, but most animals are completely unaffected.

Today, freezing for periods longer than five years is not recommended by cryogenic engineers. Cryogenic patients who have been frozen for ten years or longer are advised to undergo psychological screening on a regular basis.
