The Rank system of the [[Royal Imperial Army]] is a very traditional one, with Enlisted, Warranted and Commissioned ranks.

== Enlisted Ranks ==

Enlisted is any rating below that of a commissioned or warrant officer. The term includes non-commissioned officers. Though it is the lowest of the ranks, it is the most important. The lower-enlisted are the core of the fighting force, while the non-commissioned officers provide direct leadership and training to the soldiers.

* Private - A Private (E1) wears a single chevron.
* Private First Class - A Private First Class (E2) wears a chevron with one rocker.
* Corporal - A Corporal (E3) wears two chevrons.
* Sergeant - A Sergeant (E4) wears three chevrons.
* Staff Sergeant - A Staff Sergeant (E5) wears three chevrons and a rocker.
* Sergeant First Class - A Sergeant First Class (E6) wears three chevrons and two rockers.
* Master Sergeant - A Master Sergeant (E7) three chevrons and three rockers.
* Sergeant Major - A Sergeant Major (E8) wears three chevrons, three rockers, and a star.
* Sergeant Major of the Army - The Sergeant Major of the Army (E9) is the direct reporter and aide to the General of the Legions. The Sergeant Major of the Army wears three chevrons, three rockers, and the Prefecture emblem surrounded by two stars.

== Warrant Ranks ==

A Warranted soldier is someone with special skills needed for specific tasks that require them to act outside the usual non-commissioned chain of command, but their experience or record does not warrant a commissioned rank.

* Warrant Officer - In the Royal Imperial Army a Warrant Officer (W1) is any specialist in the Army that does not warrant a commission, and their duties do not allow them to hold a non-commissioned rank. Typically, they hold an engineering or similar non-combat position. A Warrant Officer the Imperial emblem.

== Commissioned Ranks ==

Commissioned officers are given formal orders from the Emperor, granting them their rank and privileges. This "commission" carries a heavy burden of responsibility. An officer is expected to fight, think, and lead better than any enlisted soldier.

* Second Lieutenant - In the Royal Imperial Army a Second Lieutenant (O1) is the lowest commissioned rank, one usually given to officers who have just graduated officer's training school or a military academy. A Second Lieutenant is typically in command of a Platoon-sized element. A Second Lieutenant wears a brass bar.
* First Lieutenant - In the Royal Imperial Army a First Lieutenant (O2) is the highest rank that can be granted to an officer fresh out of training, and is typically promoted from a Second Lieutenant after 18 to 24 months of service, or 2-4 months of combat service. First Lieutenants lead specialist Platoons or are second-in-command of Companies. A First Lieutenant wears a silver bar.
* Captain - In the Royal Imperial Army a Captain (O3) is generally in command of a company-sized unit. They are the lowest rank allowed to instruct at an officer's training school or academy. Because of Navy traditions, Captains are given an informal temporary promotion to "Major" during their stay on a Navy vessel: only the ship's Captain is a Captain on his or her own vessel. A Captain wears two silver bars.
* Major - In the Royal Imperial Army a Major (O5) rarely commands, and is typically second-in-command of a battalion, or on the staff of a brigade command. A Major wears a brass oak leaf.
* Lieutenant Colonel - In the Royal Imperial Army a Lieutenant Colonel (O5) is typically in command of a battalion. A Lieutenant Colonel wears a silver oak leaf.
* Colonel - In the Royal Imperial Army a Colonel (O6) is typically in command of a brigade. Traditionally, a Colonel's rank insignia is a bird with spread wings, like an eagle or a hawk. The Empire's colonels wear a wolf's head.
* Brigadier - In the Royal Imperial Army a Brigadier (O7) is in command of a Division. Ancient traditions gave the Brigadier command of a Brigade, but since the 20th century Brigadiers have held additional responsibilities. A Brigadier wears a silver star.
* Major General - In the Royal Imperial Army a Major General (O8) is either in command of a Division or a Major Command under a Legion, depending on their combat experience and service record. Major Generals also head officer training schools and academies. A Major General wears two silver stars.
* Lieutenant General - In the Royal Imperial Army a Lieutenant General (O9) is usually second in command of a Legion, commanding multiple Divisions in deployment and combat and assisting the General of the Legion in day to day affairs. A Lieutenant General wears three silver stars.
* General - In the Royal Imperial Army a General (O10) is in command of a Legion. A General wears four silver stars.
* General of the Army - In the Royal Imperial Army the General of the Army (O11) is the supreme commander of all forces in the Army. [[Cecil Au-Yueng]] was the only General of the Army. The General of the Army wears a circle of five stars.
