'''Volucris Tactics (Bug Fightin')''' As told by [[Travis Carpenter]]


Killing Volucris is not like killing people. An individual bug differs from a human in several important ways.

Firstly: a bug does not slow under fire. It is never, I repeat never, suppressed. A Nymph or Cutter under fire advances as quickly as a Nymph or Cutter taking an evening stroll: really damn fast.

The only possible way to slow a Volucris advance is to make them cross difficult terrain. Concertina wire won't cut through Volucris skin, but it will trip them up. Minefields are effective; claymores will kill the smaller breeds and can sometimes strike weak points in Tyrant or Slicer carapace. Anti-tank mines can harm larger breeds but shouldn't be relied on to put them down.

Secondly: a bug does not break and it will never run. There is no way to frighten a bug swarm into backing off; not through numbers, displays, or raw firepower. Kill the local queen, however, and bugs may be thrown into confusion.

You will always be outnumbered by the bugs. You must kill them before they reach you; when the bugs take the fighting to your troops and keep them in one spot, you will lose. The bugs can kill at range, largely through plasma fire from Bombardiers and Blisters, but this is nothing compared to their proficiency in melee combat.

Third: a wounded bug is not a dead bug. It will keep coming if it can move, and it will fight until it bleeds out. Some of the breeds are more dangerous when wounded. A big bug, particularly an Imperator, might be knocked out by hydrostatic shock only to get up later in the battle. Bugs are tough.




There are two ways to fight the bugs, then. The first is the tactic that we've used, with some success on [[Tavou]] and [[Kronheim]]: hole up and wait for them. Build defensive fighting positions and build them in depth: expect to fall back multiple times. Layer your minefields as thick as you can, but leave lanes for your forces to fall back through. Set up these retreat lines as killzones for your mortars or artillery. [[Kreig Jansen]] lost his troops during the defense of Oselo largely because his defense didn't have enough depth.

You need infantry. A rifle or [[Lansvapen]] can kill a small bug, and that frees up other guns to go after the big ones. More importantly, the infantry can defend themselves in melee; not as well as [[:Category:Rigs|Rigs]], maybe, but better than a [[:Category:Tanks|tank]]. [[Prefecture]] commanders: if it comes down to melee, you can treat your [[:Category:Rigs|Rigs]] like big infantrymen, not as armor. More importantly, though, infantry are versatile; they can engineer hasty defenses, attack or defend in rough terrain, and through good use of heavy-weapons squads make up for the lack of artillery or anti-armor weapons.

You need armor. There's nothing like a big gun to kill a big bug. Bombs and heat only knock them out. To kill an Imperator requires penetration. If you can, use projectile weapons; a lot of bugs are plasma resistant.

Armor will anchor your defenses; fight with armor but make sure you can move them quickly if necessary. Don't let your tanks be caught up close or they'll be butchered. Unless you're on open terrain, Don't bother with skirmishers like [[Ikronin]] Eldsvada; they aren't quick enough to dodge all the bug fire and they're far better used as ways to move your infantry about.

Above all, you need artillery or air support; preferably artillery, because the Volucris will probably have air superiority in their claws, making things rough for bomber pilots. It's the only way to kill them fast enough.

If your unit has organic mortars, load them with cluster bombs or napalm, to kill small breeds; leave the larger bugs to your armor.

RUN before the bugs get close. Static defenses are only good up until the bugs are on top of you. Have enough APCs to move your infantry, and move them quickly. Fall back to the next fighting position, call in artillery fire, and kill as many of them as you can. Repeat as frequently as possible.

Don't rely on terrain chokepoints to save you. The bugs will burrow, they will climb, and they will fly. Keep a reserve to cover retreats and to plug gaps in the line. If you're one of my Prefecture generals, your Rigs will be ideal for this task, and they can cavalry-charge any breaches long enough to move infantry and armor back in.

You need a supply tail. Ammunition and artillery fire must keep flowing to maintain a defense. In the end, it comes down to killing all the bugs before you run out of shells.

Don't expect to win against the bugs. Not once have the Volucris failed to bring enough expendable breeds to accomplish a goal. At least, we think they've never failed in a goal. We're not sure of the motivation behind any of their attacks so far, so we can't know if we've won or merely provided a nuisance. If the Bugs had ever brought their full numbers to bear on us, there wouldn't be anyone left to talk about it.

That, in the end, is the weakness of holding the line: the bugs can always overwhelm it. If the Swarms were made up entirely of smaller breeds, it might be possible to set up impenetrable fire sacks, but sooner or later enough Imperators or Blisters will show up that it becomes impossible to stop them all.

If we want to win against the Volucris, we have to take the fight against them: attack out of fixed defenses, hit the queens and the large bugs, then E&E before they can retaliate. This kind of warfare requires fast, survivable units with heavy firepower. Sustainability isn't needed; leave the supply train at home. The [[Ixoarchada]], [[Ikronin]], and [[Lorithos]] know how to fight this kind of war. Keep the swarm off-balance and you may prevent them from ever putting together a coherent attack. As inhuman as they may be, they do have a command-and-control system.

The last point I'll emphasize is that you cannot rely on the bugs to be predictable. Of all the aliens we've met, they're the most alien. They'll do things that don't make any sense. I've heard tales, sometimes even confirmed reports of brilliant Nymphs and Cutters, of bugs who used technological weapons, and of killer larva in the food supply on our warships. Expect the unexpected; expect the alien. Good luck.
