The Rank system of the Marine Corps is a very traditional one, with Enlisted and Commissioned ranks.

== Enlisted ==

Enlisted is any rating below that of a commissioned officer. The term includes non-commissioned officers. Though it is the lowest of the ranks, it is the most important. The lower-enlisted are the core of the fighting force, while the non-commissioned officers provide direct leadership and training to the soldiers.

* Private (E1) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps, a Private wears a single chevron.
* Lance Corporal (E2) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Lance Corporal wears a chevron with crossed rifles.
* Corporal (E3) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Corporal wears two chevrons with crossed rifles.
* Sergeant (E4) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Sergeant wears three chevrons with crossed rifles.
* Staff Sergeant (E5) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Staff Sergeant wears three chevrons and a rocker with crossed rifles
* Gunnery Sergeant (AKA "Gunny") (E6) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Gunnery Sergeant wears three chevrons and two rockers with crossed rifles.
* Master Sergeant (E7) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Master Sergeant wears three chevrons and three rockers with crossed rifles.
* Sergeant Major (E8) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Sergeant Major three chevrons, three rockers, and the Marine logo.
* Sergeant Major of the Corps (E9) - The Sergeant Major of the Marine Corps is the direct reporter and aide to the Commandant of the Marines. The Sergeant Major of the Army wears three chevrons, three rockers, and the Marine emblem surrounded by two stars.

== Commissioned ==

Commissioned officers are given formal orders from the Emperor, granting them their rank and privileges. This "commission" carries a heavy burden of responsibility. An officer is expected to fight, think, and lead better than any enlisted soldier.

* Ensign (O1) -  In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps an Ensign is the lowest commissioned rank, one usually given to officers who have just graduated officer's training school or a military academy. An Ensign is typically in command of a Platoon-sized element. An Ensign wears a brass bar.
* Lieutenant (O2) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Lieutenant is the highest rank that can be granted to an officer fresh out of training, and is typically promoted from an Ensign after 18 to 24 months of service, or 2-4 months of combat service. Lieutenants lead specialist Platoons or are second-in-command of Companies. A Lieutenant wears a silver bar.
* Captain (O3) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Captain is generally in command of a company-sized unit. They are the lowest rank allowed to instruct at an officer's training school or academy. Because of Navy traditions, Captains are given an informal temporary promotion to "Major" during their stay on a Navy vessel: only the ship's Captain is a Captain on his or her own vessel. A Captain wears two silver bars.
* Major (O4) -  In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Major (O5) rarely commands, and is typically second-in-command of a battalion, or on the staff of a brigade command. A Major wears a brass oak leaf.
* Lieutenant Colonel (O5) -  In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Lieutenant Colonel (O5) is typically in command of a battalion. A Lieutenant Colonel wears a silver oak leaf.
* Colonel (O6) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Colonel (O6) is typically in command of a brigade. Traditionally, a Colonel's rank insignia is a bird with spread wings, like an eagle or a hawk. The Empire's colonels wear a silver wolf's head.
* Brigadier (O7) - In the Royal Imperial Marine Corps a Brigadier (O7) is in command of a Division. Ancient traditions gave the Brigadier command of a Brigade, but since the 20th century Brigadiers have held additional responsibilities. A Brigadier wears a silver star. Because of the [[Onyx Empire]]'s use of the Marines as a purely defensive force, their size makes the need for any rank higher than Brigadier unneccessary.
