==Prefecture Rifles==
The overhaul of the infantry during the Modern Warfare Initiative of  
2325 brought about many changes in the weapons they used. One major  
change was the Main Battle Rifle. 
  
The Prefecture had several requirements. 
The rifle was to be no more than 65 centimeters in length, and weigh  
no more than 7 kilograms when fully loaded. 
It must have a gauss acceleration system capable of launching a 12mm  
slug up to 1200 meters per second. 
It must fire in single-fire, 4-round burst, and full automatic. 
It must fire at least 1000 rounds per minute. 
  
  
Two companies submitted designs for consideration: Achekmatov- 
Garkavyi from Holt, and Kodiak Arms of Tavou. 
  
The Achekmatov-Garkavyi Model 61 Prototype was submitted for testing  
to all four branches of the Prefecture military. It was a large  
design, capable of firing at 2000 meters per second and 1200 rounds  
per minute. It accepted up to a 200-round belt-fed magazine, and  
had an optional 20mm grenade launcher. The final product, the AG-61  
MBR, was accepted by the Navy and the Air Force for their special  
forces. 
  
The Kodiak Arms Model 18 Prototype was only submitted to the Army  
and the Marine Corps for testing. It was a smaller design, only  
60cm long. It fired rounds at 1500 meters per second, and 1000  
rounds per minute. It accepted 50 round magazines and 100 round  
drum belts. 
In numbers alone, the K-18 was an inferior rifle. The Tavou company  
had a special addition: variable ammunition. 
For close-range combat, a soldier could use 12mm "flechette"  
ammunition. Every 10 meters of flight, the round would break in  
three pieces. After more than 30 meters, the shrapnel was too small  
to be effective, but at close range it was devastating. 
For mid-range combat, the extremely popular "breecher" round was  
available. This round featured a secondary chemical propellant and  
an explosive head with proximity sensors. Upon nearing the point of  
impact, the chemical propellant ignites, tripling the velocity of  
the round. It explodes just after penetrating, to an incredible  
effect. Breechers had been in use for a long time, but were not  
available on a standard rifle before. 
The last round was a super-sonic sabot-stabilized round. It was  
effective at extreme ranges, and had microsensors built in to  
compensate for wind interference. 
The final product was designated the K-18 MBR. The Marine Corps  
accepted the K-18, but the Army is awaiting results from an  
undisclosed third bidder before they make their final decision.
