&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Grand Cathedral of [[Stone Bridge]] was filled far beyond capacity. On any given day, over twelve thousand [[Mesarthrim]] would crowd inside for daily Mass. Today was different. Today, there was barely room to breathe, with over forty thousand Mesarthrim men, women and children packed inside. Today was the day that [[Andreas Tischler]] would be laid to rest.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;For the Mesarthrim people, there was no greater hero than Andreas. He had freed them from slavery. He gave them dignity. He brought them back to God. He was their priest-king, their warrior-poet, the paragon of what it meant to ''be'' Mesarthrim. The years under Andreas Tischler's rule, as short as they had been, were already known as the Mesarthrim Golden Age.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It has been said that Mesarthrim only shed tears of blood. To a normal person, the savage ferocity of a Mesarthrim can make them seem more animal than human. Their emotional armor, forged in a culture of violence, may seem impenetrable. It is not. For all their warmongering, the Mesarthrim are still humans. There is not a dry eye among those gathered here today. Today, they were like children, laying their father to rest. The tears they cry are like any other.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The floor of the cathedral was built like a great basin, descending nearly sixty meters in a series of smooth steps. In the center was a raised dais, where the body of Andreas Tischler lay in a coffin of carved mahogany. The mortal remains of the great hero were left almost exactly as they were at the moment of his death. His body was flash-frozen minutes after death. His death-wound still oozed blood. The grime and sweat of his final battlefield still caked his face. His torn uniform and sundered armor still covered his flesh, useless now. The grim moment of death itself was perfectly preserved; a hideous, pervasive sense of the fleeting mortality of ''now''--a grisly reminder of what it ''truly'' meant to be Mesarthrim.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The body was not alone on the dais. At the base of the dais were the Myt'gaul--''my blood''--the elite guards, the best of the Mesarthrim military. As much as they loved their leader, there was no telling what the people would do in their grief. Only days ago, they had been ready to go to war with an innocent ally. The Myt'gaul continued to serve their leader, even after his death. No harm would come to the body or to Andreas' friends.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There was also another man, standing at the pinacle of the dais. This man was not one of the honor guard. He was there to eulogize the fallen hero.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The choice of speaker had been a thorn in the side of the [[Clan Lords]]. When Andreas' will had been read aloud in front of their assembly, there had nearly been a riot. With the Mesarthrim population at a boiling point, they had hoped for someone to appease the masses. Someone like Andreas' sister would have been best. The Clan Lords would have also preferred [[Arnold Stadt]], Andreas' most loyal supporter, and a Clan Lord himself. Even someone like that upstart, [[Steven Cue]], would have helped to pacify the rabble of [[Caledon]]. Anyone would have been preferable to this ''foreigner''.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Despite their misgivings, the will had been clear. If word got out that the Clan Lords defied the will of Andreas Tischler, even in death, it would mean the end of them. The Clans would fall. So with clenched teeth and suppressed bile, they sent for the man who stood on the dais today.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;His name was [[Scott Sanburn]]. Among his own people, he was a hero in his own right; a great leader and statesman, who had fought against an empire. Despite the titles he held and the honors he had earned, he wore only a simple blue dress uniform, along with the rank pins for the office of "General-Commander".<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sanburn looked down quietly at the face of the man he had called "friend." Andreas' light brown hair was matted with blood and dirt. His cheeks were still slightly colored, though it was fading fast. His expression was peaceful. Despite the agony of his wounds, Andreas had died with the hint of a smile on his lips. "Hello again, Andreas." Sanburn softly greeted the dead man.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sanburn raised his gaze, and looked at the throng gathered in honor of their fallen leader. He held a prepared speech in his hands, which had already been translated into the local German dialect--a needless gesture, as most Mesarthrim understood English--but he no longer planned to use it. Sanburn cleared his throat, and began, determined to speak from the heart.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"Friends..." his voice cracked, and he looked down again, pausing for several moments before continuing. "Friends. We have come together in our sorrow to mourn the passing of a great leader. You all looked up to him. He was your captain, your hero, and your father. He was none of that to me, but he was my friend. While we are all united in our grief, I hope you can join me in the celebration of the life of a great man.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"I first met Andreas Tischler at the height of the conflict we now call the [[Second Exodus War]]. When your people encountered the [[Onyx Empire]], they recognized them for the tyrants they were, and joined us in the struggle against them. Andreas led your forces in defense of my home, and the other worlds that had been united under the banner of [[Earth]].<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"Until you Mesarthrim joined our cause, I was nearly ready to despair. The Empire was winning. Even when Andreas and his forces joined the fight, we were outnumbered and outgunned. Andreas didn't care. He didn't know the meaning of fear. He took the fight to them, drove them off every world, and beat them in every engagement like it was practice. Every engagement until [[Cronus]].<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"Cronus was a deathtrap. The enemy was dug in so deeply that we could have bombarded them for decades without making a dent in their defenses. We were planning on just bottling them up, but Andreas insisted on bringing the fight to the enemy. Despite my misgivings, I supported him. The night before the attack, I asked him; 'Andreas, the enemy has five soldiers for every one we can put in the field. Even if we manage to win this, good men are going to die. What are you thinking? What do you hope to do?'<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"But Andreas told me, 'We are going to do good. And we're going to win. Our enemies will always outnumber us, but if we allow fear to rule our actions, our defeat is inevitable.' And the next day, he led us to victory. That's what made him great. Andreas was afraid of nothing. He knew he would die one day, and embraced his fate willingly. He had a spirit of fire, which burned away the shadows of doubt in our hearts, and melted away the fear that froze our souls."<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sanburn's voice died down, until it was barely above a whisper, so soft that none of the crowd could hear him. "I just don't know why he fought so hard to uphold a culture of violence." He stepped down from the dais, and began to make his way out of the cathedral. "He was a hero!" he shouted as he went. "Never forget him!"<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There was nothing left to say.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Andreas Tischler lay in state for two days. His remains were cremated, and his ashes were scattered across the atmosphere of [[Mesar]].
