Gravity Cavitation Drive

The Phoenix Project's drive system is based on the new gravity projection system developed for Gradient Gravity Shielding. The developers of the drive system were told to meet two specifications. One: create a drive system that can hook directly to a fusion reactor and require no additional fuel source. This requirement keeps resupply time to a minimum. Two: the drive system must operate well above the parameters of the Impulse Engine, and have no moving parts. This requirement keeps maintenance to a minimum. Originally, the project was called the Reactionless Drive System (RDS). The idea was to create a hole in space-time, and "fall" into it, closing the hole before the ship ceased to exist. Plans of this sort were soon dismissed as impossible with current technology--and later mathematically proved to be completely impossible, now or ever.
The new and final plan for the GCD works on a principle of orbiting a virtual gravity well. Virtual gravity, like the principles used in gravity shields, is based on a Tensor model of gravitational physics instead of a Vector model. Tensors are very much like vectors--in fact, vectors are tensors. Scalars are 0-Rank tensors, vectors are 1-Rank tensors, a standard number matrix is a 2-Rank tensor, and so on. Tensors have numerous properties that are very useful for encoding information in nonlinear systems. In most situations, Tensor Model and Vector Model gravity is identical. In carefully controlled environments, certain factors of Tensor Model gravity can be used to accomplish things that were thought impossible in earlier physics theories. By using Tensor-model virtual gravity, developers of the gravity drive were able to calculate a standard matrix where all numbers, except the standard diagonal coordinate system, are zero. Once this state is known, it is possible to shift the diagonal coordinates to a point where the gravity behaves differently. This shift allows the gravity drive to create a virtual gravity field, which the ship is pulled towards. The ship travels along an orbital path around a point in "state space." State space is the state of the tensor variables, given spatial properties to allow for visual perception. For example, vector and tensor models can be compared by using two state space models. First, the simplest system, the switch. A switch has only two states, On and Off. This can be described as a state of 0 or 1. With a tensor, the example becomes a dimmer switch, which has nearly infinite states between 0 and 1. The states the Gravity Cavitation Drive is concerned with are the amplitude of the off-diagonal components of the tensor field, and the vector components of the ship's mass. Normally, mass is scalar and gravity is vector; but in the drive's state space mass is a vector and gravity is a tensor, which is multiplied to get the net effect. The change in the vector/tensor quantities appears as a conic section. As the ship gains velocity, the gravity field must decrease in energy. If the ship decelerates (going against the field), the field gains energy. The ship can actually reverse course with minimal loss of velocity and gravity field energy, but only below certain velocities, and depending on the mass of the ship. The conic section causes the ship to oscillate wildly through state space, due to the instability of the pathway. The gravity field is inverse squared--the amount of force the ship can use to accelerate depends on how often a new virtual gravity field is created. The Tensor model of gravity behaves very similar to the vector model, as the off-diagonal components are usually zero. It is possible, however, to manipulate gravity such that the tensor is skewed, and attracts the ship, but does not attract other mass. Because the relationship between gravitational mass and inertial mass is linear, inertia is not an issue to the crew of the ship. A sudden change in direction is not felt at all by the crew. On a planet, where gravity is constant, changes in direction cause inertial forces to be felt. In space travel, the only source of gravity is the gravity drive itself. The pull of the virtual gravity field is a uniform force, that is, a force felt identically by all parts of the ship at the same time in the same concentration. Every single atom of and on the vessel is accelerated in exactly the same direction at the same time, so inertia is never felt. Vessels that expect to fight battles or make complicated maneuvers must install Gravity Stabilizer Couplings. When a ship is impacted by a weapon blast, or spins suddenly to bring its weapons to bear, inertial forces do come into play, but only partially. All ships equipped with Gradient Gravity Shielding automatically accommodate the shift in geometric state changes.
