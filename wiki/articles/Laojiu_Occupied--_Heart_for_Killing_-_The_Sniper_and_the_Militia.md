by Justin Rahn

Janggok, Casere, Laojiu District
     The intermittent flickers of a distant plains thunderstorm break through the nighttime darkness and scatter about the ground through the leaves and branches making up the forest’s canopy.
     The ground and air are damp from the now long away storm as Aloysius, with his twenty-pound tactical suit and gun, crawls through the dense foliage and soft soil in an attempt to find the best possible position from which to stalk his prey.
     Ah, finally, somewhere with a decent view and a decent amount of coverage he thinks, as he eyes the massive tree rocketing skyward, flashing in the distant blue-hued lightening.
     Unbuckling his rough sack and what little gear he has with him, he begins to climb tree with the confidence and speed of a seasoned lumberjack, finally coming to a halt about thirty feet up. With a couple of twists and contortions, he’s secured to the tree and ready to set up the “hunting stand”, as the instructors at sniper school called it.
     So much better than Dad’s, don’t have to worry about this one getting jammed...damn thing never wanted to work... the thoughts trail off to home as he operates on autopilot to secure the stand around the rough bark of the tree.
     Finally, after latching all the necessary ropes, it’s secure; he could tell just by the way it hung from the tree when after letting go for the first time. It’ll support my weight, I’m not that heavy to begin with and these things are rated to bear over 200 kilos.

     Aloysius is truly impressed with the view upon taking a seat and resting his arms against his sides. He can see everything within at least 500 yards, even more with zoom, though he can’t see directly behind the tree, but the camera feed into his helmet should let him know of any movement behind the stand.
     Well, enough gawking, I need to get to work. Climbing down the tree he thinks about the mission that has been given to the squad directly from the high command, which is very intimidating at first, but kind of flattering.
     Switching on the video feed, he watches the in-vehicle recordings in order to be clear what happened: Prefecture militiamen attacked a convoy of combat supplies and support vehicles, killing more than a dozen men, and then seamlessly blended back into the forest from which they came. He switches to the audio recording of the orders.
     “Sergeant Reinhardt, your scout squad has been ordered to provide detailed and up to date information concerning a possible underground tunnel system in the forest off of route five zero one, where our supply lines were attacked yesterday.
     All of the information that you need, from maps to video feeds, is being uploaded as we speak to each of your scouts’ PBCs [Personal Battlefield Computers]. If your men encounter the enemy, observe and relay any tactical information back to headquarters for further analysis, then eliminate any immediate threats and call for extraction.
     Remember, complete radio silence. The only feed base wants is data, there is no need for your men to expose themselves with excess electronic spillover.”
     Detailed information, underground tunnels, radio silence, eliminate threats. Aloysius repeats the orders to be sure he has them down. The first time in the field can be a little overwhelming and school can only prepare you so much, but in the end, real life is something almost wholly different.
     Finally making it back up to the perch with the gear, he sits down and takes out the rifle case, opens it, and begins to quickly assemble the variety of parts, as only a well-conditioned soldier could.
     As the pieces come together, it is clear why members of the scout squads are some of the most feared sharpshooters in human space: their sleek rifles, adaptive camouflage, and passionless killer instincts make them the best snipers in the Terran sector, able to find and eliminate their prey with almost mechanical precision, without remorse or guilt.
     With a satisfying click of the scope attaching to the top of the body, Aloysius’ rifle is finally complete and ready to eliminate any Prefecture soldier or militiaman that come within range. Looking over the weapon to make sure everything is in its place, he takes off the combat goggles and peers through the scope.
     Aiming at the furthest spot he can see, the advanced viewfinder does it job beautifully, displaying the range of 850 meters, a light wind of 3 miles per hour, and an amazingly detailed picture of the target area. I love not having to calculate all this by hand...God, that was a pain in the ass.
     With the equipment working as well as could be wished and the flicker of lightning dying as the distant storm moves off, Aloysius turns on the sound detection equipment and hunkers down for a long night.
     It’s 0200 and Aloysius has been watching all around the site for the past four hours and has seen nothing...absolutely nothing. With a long sigh, he rearranges his tired body. Arm’s asleep...never taught us how to deal with that...oh well...guess there’s just some thing’s one must learn by himself...
     Thumbing through the various channels of the scope, from infrared, through visible, into ultraviolet, something catches his ear through the audio equipment, almost sounding like a splash. Looking through the scope to where it came from, what he sees sends a little jolt through his spine. Wow, my first targets...and they look to be militiamen, judging from what they’re carrying...maybe this’ll be a decent night after all.
     Toying with the microphone setup and using some crafty finger work, the omnidirectional mic is now transformed into an ultra sensitive unidirectional sound detector, giving Aloysius an unprecedented degree of intimacy into a conversation that has just begun...

     “Seth, come on...it’s 0150, we need to go topside and patrol...make sure everything’s secure so that no white-eyes find our little hide out...that wouldn’t make anyone’s life easier,” Thomas Brooks bellows down the stark, concrete gray corridor that makes up one of the arms of the massive maze that is the militia’s headquarters, buried deep beneath the soil.
     In the distance he can hear the running footsteps and then, from around the corner, pops the tall, fit figure of a young man, panting from the obvious sprint just completed from the barracks.
     “Sorry sir...I must have lost track of time...I was cleaning my weapon...I didn’t realize...“
     “It’s alright, we still have a couple of minutes before we’re scheduled to be up there...I just wanted you to be ready, this is your first time out,” Tom says, reassuringly patting the young man on the back.
     “And what did I tell you about calling me ‘sir’...just call me Tom...we aren’t part of the military, so stop with the formalities.”
     “Sorry Mr. Brooks, it won’t happen again...just getting caught up in the atmosphere of the war...” Seth’s bleak words trail off as his voice echoes through the maze, giving the whole environment a creepy feel of a real life horror movie.

     The two men walk single file down the hall until coming to a ladder leading topside, the only telltale sign of its path skyward is the substantial puddle that has formed on the floor from the heavy rains that poured down earlier in the day.
     Looking toward to ceiling and starting to climb up the old fashioned rope ladder, Tom signals for Seth to strap his rifle across his back and follow. 
     “The only way to do this is to just jump in with both feet...they probably aren’t even out there, so don’t worry about it...we’re only going out for a look.”
     Seth grabs his rifle and straps it around his chest, allowing it to comfortably rest on his back, and checks one more time to make sure he grabbed everything he needed, patting his front and finally his back pockets.
     Ok, I did grab it...good, that’ll keep me going, gives me something I know I can live for. Letting out a satisfying sigh, he smiles for the first time tonight and climbs the ladder skyward.
     Lifting the light wood plank door and peeking out of the small, manhole cover size exit, a sudden gust of wind sweeps through the forest, allowing the mist laden leaves to release their unbearable cargo, sending large, heavy raindrops hurtling to the ground, catching Tom in eye before he has a chance to blink.
     With a quick glance around the perimeter of the hole, he steps out onto the soft ground, moving in a crouched position, circling the hole before motioning the Seth to continue the assent up to the jet black night that is above him.
     Finally fully standing, Tom looks over to where Seth is peeking his head up. “It’s clear, you can come out without having to worry about getting shot, my scanners didn’t pick up anything and I didn’t see anyone...not unusual, who would want to be out in this weather anyway...come on, lets go see why the relays on the fritz.”
     Ok Seth, you can do this, just a couple of more steps and then you’re out...from there you just have to check the relay connection and do a quick perimeter patrol...then you’re done, that’s it, back to the safety of the barracks. Seth, shaking with a combination of nerves and adrenaline makes the final few steps out of the hole, allowing the damp, dark night to envelope him like a cocoon.

     The night is black, the air is heavy with moisture, and the soil is damp from the rejuvenating storm that swept through the area today and gave the farmers the much-needed rain for their crops. The two men, single file, walk through the densely covered forest floor in search of the communication’s relay station.
     “Man, that must have been a pretty good storm that swept through here, too bad we weren’t topside to watch the fireworks...that’s the only problem with being underground...you miss some of the fun stuff...but at least we only have to be here on weekends...keeps some sense of normalcy I guess...what do you think Mr. Brooks?”
     “Yeah, I’ll agree with you on that point, you might miss some stuff, but not the important things in life, like your family or schooling. Speaking of, how’s that been going for you?”
     “Which one, the family or the schooling Mr. Brooks?” Seth says, trailing off the question, pondering what he’s going to say about either one and not coming up with much.
     “Both, being that each of them should be equally important to a young man such as yourself,” Tom says, turning his head to look Seth in the eyes.
     Unfortunately, in the behest to both look Seth in the eyes as well as read the map, Tom trips over a fallen branch and splashes down into a modest puddle, soaking his already half drenched uniform.
     Muttering curses under his breadth as Seth comes to help, Tom is eventually up and the conversation continues like it never stopped in the first place.
     “Well, the family I can’t complain about, everyone’s doin’ good, and I’m loving college, it’s finally someplace I can learn something useful and have fun doing it...other than that, nothing really earth shattering...how about you Mr. Brooks...ummm...how’s...uh...your family doing?”
     Seth’s voice easily gives away the uncertainly and awkwardness about asking the question.

     Aloysius, now following their every footstep with mechanical precision, is caught off guard by the content of the conversation. Who talks about daily life while on patrol...guess that’s the difference between real soldiers and militiamen.
     However, still hoping to gather some valuable intelligence that could be useful in crushing the enemy and out of just curiosity, he continues to track them in the sight and listen to their every word.

     “Well, Sara still has at least a week to go...you remember how middle school works...and Alexia and Casey are pretty much done, though I think they both have a couple of finals next week...but other than that, they’re finished...”
     “Yeah, and Lex is done period...from what I can remember she seemed kind of hesitant about graduating...she really loved that school...” Seth’s mind trails off thinking about a year ago, the last day of high school, something he couldn’t wait for, he just wanted out, loved the teachers but hated the school. I still don’t understand how anyone could enjoy an environment of academic atrophy...oh well, guess that might have been part it...not the same level of pride.
     “She still is...though I think she’s accepted it...it’s a big transition, especially at seventeen, to be moving away to college...most kids do it at eighteen and they’re usually only barely ready then.”
     “At least she’s going to where she wanted...it was amazing to see her face when she told me about getting accepted...she looked so happy and relieved...” Seth’s words trail off into the cold air, moist air.
     He has a habit of doing that, especially when it comes to the emotional stuff, too analytical, too thought driven...not enough feeling. He sighs, attempting to find the words he wants to say...the awkward silence between the men is finally broken.
     “Mr. Brooks,” Seth begins, slightly hesitant to finish the sentence and it’s easy to see, “look...I’m really sorry if my letter caused...any kind of trouble for your family...it wasn’t my intention to spark any kind of conflict...I just needed to let Lex know what was going on in my head...and the best way I could do that was by writing...give her some time and space to think it over...it just didn’t go the way I thought it would...”
     Never does...should have chosen different words...might have conveyed the information better...might have provoked a different reaction...I write like a lawyer, even to the people I love. Seth laughs at the utter stupidity. ‘...like a chimeric hybrid of a thesis paper and a love letter...’ who the hell writes that and gives it to their girlfriend...
     Another awkward silence envelops the two men, each thinking about what has just been said, pondering what the response will be, contemplating what needs to be said and conveyed.
     He’s a good kid...no, a good young man...treated her like the young lady she is...never had a problem with him, always brought her home on time, never late...a truly caring gentlemen...but I still blew up in his face, Tom thinks to himself, starring off into the distance, following the map and occasionally looking down at the GPS screen that shimmers a bright blue tinge onto his face.
     “We have another couple of yards and the communications relay is over there, next to that tree...it’s a small box, about the size of a toaster oven, buried about a foot underground...” Tom says, breaking the awkward silence that had persisted for the last half minute and then motions over to his left, to where the relay should be.

     Aloysius, perched in high in the hunting stand is both confused and intrigued by the conversation, something about it strikes an ancient chord in him, something that he feels in the outskirts of his being...knowing that feeling about when things just don’t go the way you think they will or should, especially when it comes to those you love...it’s almost a kind of empathy.
     However, though mulling over the teen’s apology about a letter, he is not distracted enough by the thoughts to miss the pair crouch down and begin digging next to a fairly large tree, like squirrels looking for buried nuts.
     The high command will love this...it would cripple the militias communication ability and would render them blind to the rest of the world...easily dealt with after that. Aloysius does some more magical finger work and is recording video through the scope of where they’re digging, as well as judging the distance and location according to the preloaded map of the area.
     He watches intently as the two men excavate the hole and peer in. After a couple of minutes of fiddling they cover the hole back up and place some sticks and leaves, primitive camouflage, over the newly tilled earth.

     “Wow, that’s kind of funny, guess it just needed to be cuffed a couple of times to get it working again...stupid thing, we always get the cheapest equipment...I guess it works when it needs to though,” Tom finishes up placing sticks and leaves over the newly disturbed patch of earth lying at his feet.
     Not completely knowing how to handle the situation that has presented itself, Tom tries his best to sooth the youth’s nervousness, “Look, Seth...don’t beat yourself up over it...some stuff just comes to an end, sometimes when we least expect it...it wasn’t either of your faults...it was just the time and place for it to end.”
     “I know that...but it just doesn’t seem fair...I did everything right...the random roses I surprised her with...the dinners I cooked...everything women say they want...and I was upfront and honest with my feelings...I didn’t hide behind any kind of macho, jerk image.
     “I just don’t know where it went wrong...I swear I did everything right...” The words, quiet and humble, roll off his the tongue and into the darkness.
     Seth sits down on the nearest damp, moss covered log and peers out into the darkness around him, confused as ever about why he’s talking like this to her father, so open and so honest. I guess tactfulness takes a backseat to my mindless babbling about my feelings...that was probably part of it too...not knowing when to stop talking...
     “I’m also worried I hurt her somehow...screwing up any plans she had...like having a guaranteed prom date...or worse yet, betraying the trust we worked so hard to build between us...”
     A slight whimper has edged into Seth’s voice, an obvious lump in the throat has formed and he is doing everything he can to suppress its affects, but to no avail; a small tear graces his left cheek and continues on past the jaw line and onto his neck, where it combines with the sweat and rain, becoming indistinguishable from the rest of the moisture.
     “And to top it all off...talking with her after she got the letter...she said that a break was fine...and that ‘hey, people usually get back together’...but to show up at her track meet to watch...running into her there and not seeing that surprised sparkle of happiness in her eyes...that look of ‘he’s so sweet’ like she had at the swim meets...that’s the hardest thing of all...having that hope crushed...”
     The slight whimper in Seth’s voice has turned into a soft sob and any attempts at delaying the emotional outburst until back underground have failed. Cupping his face in his hands, his shoulders heave in unison as the tears start to stream down his cheeks, tears of lost hope, confusion, and fear.

     Peering through his scope, Aloysius watches in aw as the militiaman breaks down and softly cries, muttering something not quite understandable between the sobs and deep breadths. He tries to remember the last time he cried, but the memory escapes him, it wasn’t something his father tolerated. ‘Big boys don’t cry...it shows weakness and we are never to show weakness...especially in front of other men.’
    But something strange happens as he continues to peer through the scope and listen to the now more controlled sobs of the militiaman; he begins to think about home and about Hanna.
     Wow, it’ll be a year and half coming up in July...hopefully she’s doing ok back home...heard from her last week...she was almost done with school...that’s right, just had finals to finish.
     Suddenly, he’s startled by the thought of loosing her, and contemplates what life would be like, something he doesn’t want to consider any longer than needed.
     Ignoring the growing lump in his throat, another startling thought strikes him just then. What would happen to her if she lost me, He allows the notion to linger as briefly as possible, sweeping it away into the dark recess of his psyche. I have all of the intelligence I’m going to get from these two.
     The problem is they are merely fifty yards away, easily within hearing distance of someone climbing down a tree, along with the clanking of gear and rustling of branches.
     I guess this is what you would consider an immediate threat needing to be eliminated. With that thought Aloysius rests a leather-clad finger on the trigger and takes aim at the now composed militiaman.

     Finally composing himself long enough to stand up and wiping away the remaining tears from under his eyes and nose, Seth begins to slowly walk through the dense foliage, contemplating what has just happened.
     “I’m sorry for my outburst Mr. Brooks...it was by no means a show of professionalism...and to say all of that to of you, her father...it’s just depressing...knowing that the spark is gone...and the love will never be reciprocated...”
     Leaning up against a rough covered tree, Tom lets a small smile slip through his, until now, stoic demeanor. “You know...she still has your prom picture up in her room and thought about giving you a call last week to see how you were doing...she still does love you, but she just needs some time and space to reflect on what she wants in life right now...remember, there is two years between you two.”
     Slowly turning around to look at Tom, Seth searches his pants, finally pulling out a wallet from the back left pocket. Opening it up, he reaches beneath his license, taking out a small two by four inch photo.
     “I took this out of my wallet the night we broke up...didn’t seem right to have a picture of her on me when we weren’t together...I mean, what would be the point, I wasn’t going to talk about her other than to say we broke up...”

     Aloysius is caught off guard by the militiaman’s shifting hands, a million thoughts race through his mind. Does he see me...no, that can’t be...his back's to me...but what is he doing. With each passing moment, he presses a little harder on the trigger, until the militiaman begins to talk.
     Through the scope, a serene scene; it’s a picture of a couple, a tall, fit young lady in a pink dress, with her hair up and a young man, slightly shorter and just as fit, in a black tux, standing hand in hand, smiling, with a corsage on her wrist and a boutonnière on his lapel. It’s his prom photo...and that’s the girl he’s been talking about...she’s beautiful.
     His finger shakily attempts to squeeze the one-eighth inch thick piece of metal to fire the gun, but images of the young lady being held by her mother, hugging her tightly, crying on her shoulder, suddenly streak though his mind.
     Taking a shaking finger off the trigger, he presses the safety button into the on position and with a wave of terror washing over him, starts to frantically feel each of his pockets. Where is it...I know I brought it with me...which pocket...which one...it’s got to be here...I grabbed it...I know I did...it has to be here.
     After several seconds of distraught searching, relief finally finds him as he fingers the top left front pocket of the tactical vest, the one nearest his heart. Between two leather-clad fingers, he pulls a two by four inch picture out and cautiously holds it in front of him, making sure to protect it from the drops of water falling from the leaves.
     Looking down, he sees a face of the purest beauty and thinks back to when he last saw her, only a week ago while calling home. Her beauty hasn’t changed one bit since she gave me this last fall, before training.
     Carefully slipping the photo back into his vest pocket, he looks through the scope and sees the young man still standing, holding the photo.
     That’s the one thing they warned us about in training...attachment...spend enough time looking through a scope at people and you start to identify with them...you learn their names, what they look like, who they love...what they live for...
     But I to have something to live for...someone back home. And with that thought, Aloysius positions a now steady finger onto the trigger and trains his eye on the young man who is still holding the picture.

     “But when they invaded I put it back...thought it would help give me some motivation to stay ali...” Seth’s sentence is cut short as he drops the photo and grabs at his chest, hit by a well-place blazer shot, stumbling to reach the ground, finally collapsing against a tree.
     Taken off guard, Tom doesn’t have any time to react and is pierced by another well-placed blazer shot to the chest, falling where he stands, into a heap, the blaster still strapped to his back.

     Reeling from the violent scene played out through the scope, a sharp contrast to the relative peacefulness that he has been viewing for the past ten minutes, Aloysius turns the safety on and drops his gun.
     What have I done...I killed two men who had families...one almost like me who had someone he loved...to hope for...to live for.
     Running a leather-clad hand through his hair, he can feel the lump in his throat start to form and taking out Hanna’s photo again, Aloysius allows himself to cry for the first he can remember, the humble sobs piercing the otherwise quiet woods.
     After composing himself long enough to climb down the tree, equipment and all, he slowly begins the job of packing everything into a couple of small bags, light enough to include all the essentials, but still kind of awkward to carry.
     Leaving the packed bags on the ground, he walks the 50 yards to where the two militiamen lay, fallen, silent, dead. Peering down at the forest floor something catches his trained eye, something slightly out of place.
     Crouching down to examine the object, it becomes obvious what it is, the young militiaman’s prom picture, slightly dirty from being dropped, but intact nonetheless. Aloysius picks it up and wipes the dirt and crud from the picture, examining it and then flipping it over.
     Luckily for him the Mesarthrim school system requires its students to master not only their native tongue of German, but also English. He begins to read the note written on the back quietly to himself.
     “Dear Seth, Thank you from the deepest part of my heart for the best night of my life, it was amazing. I can’t wait to do this again next year and we’ll make it an even better night than this one. Love always, Lex.”
     Swallowing the lump in his throat, Aloysius slowly walks over to the militiaman’s body sitting up against a tree. Taking the left hand, he places the photo into the open palm, closes the fingers around it, and places the fist on to the lap. Finally, he closes the open, motionless eyes, allowing the valiant, caring soldier to rest peacefully.
     Upon standing and surveying the scene, he bows his head and prays, “Allmächtiger und barmherziger Gott, ich empfehlen zu Ihnen, Seth, Ihr Diener, In Ihrer Gnade und Liebe, beschmieren mit Tinte aus allen Sünden, die er durch menschliche Schwäche eingesetzt hat, In dieser Welt ist er gestorben: lassen Sie ihn leben mit Sie ewig, Ich frage dies durch Christus unseren Herrn, Amen.” (All-powerful and merciful God, I commend to you, Seth, your servant. In your mercy and love, blot out all the sins he has committed through human weakness. In this world he has died: let him live with you forever. I ask this through Christ our Lord, Amen.)
     Dabbing his eyes with the sleeve of the tactical suit, Aloysius readies himself to go, already having called for extraction as he was packing the gear. She should know what he truly thought about her...it would only be right.
     With that thought, he pushes a button on the personal battlefield computer and takes a small data chip out its housing, allowing the spring loaded door to shut itself.
     Walking back to the bodies, he opens the top left pocket on the militiaman’s bloodied uniform, carefully placing the chip inside and buttoning the pocket back up.
     “That way, she’ll know how deeply you cared for her and what she means to you...even now,” he says quietly to the motionless body lying next to the tree.

     Picking up the telephone, Lex dials the numbers easily, never having to pause to remember any of the digits and allows it to ring.
     “Hello”
     “Hi Mrs. Collins, it’s Lex, Seth home by any chance?”
     “I’m sorry dear, no he isn’t, is there anything that I can tell him for you?”
     “If you could just ask him to call me back when he gets in, though not if it’s too late, that would be fine.”
     “Ok, I’ll tell him when it gets in and you know him, if he doesn’t call you tonight, he’ll probably give you a call tomorrow.”
     “Yeah, I know, thanks a lot Mrs. Collins, have a good night.”
     “You too dear, goodnight.”
     “Bye”
     Hanging up the phone, she slumps onto her bed, slightly disappointed that he wasn’t in. Hopefully he didn’t mind me not saying good-bye to him at the track meet...didn’t think he’d leave until I was back...but it was good to see him...I guess he still cares about me...oh well, I know he’ll call back...always does.

     Recognizing the familiar tone anywhere, Lex opens her weary eyes and rolls over, moving a couple pillows out of the way, to check the time. Who in the world is at our door at 10:45 at night...maybe it’s Dad surprising us.
     With that thought, she sits up, puts her slippers on and begins wandering down the hall, when she hears her mother open the door and a man’s voice, but it’s not her father’s.
     Reaching the bottom of the stairs, Lex is confused by the fairly large man, wearing khakis pants and a polo shirt, who is standing in the doorway, holding something resembling a manila envelope.
     Adding to the confusion is her mother’s obvious nervousness, visible in a slight shaking as she clutches the doorknob. 
     “Mom...mom...what’s the matter...what’s going on?” Lex says, with a slight quake in her voice as she rushes to her mother’s side, hugging it tightly, trying to fight back the tears of confusion and fear.
     “Mrs. Brooks, I’m sorry.” With that, the man hands her mother the manila envelope, salutes and walks back down the driveway to a waiting car, which, as he closes the door, drives off down the road and stops fives houses down, at Seth’s place.
     A sudden terror washes over Lex as she holds onto mother who hugs her as tightly as she ever has.
     “He’s not going to call back is he mom?” she says, through the lump forming in her throat, as the tears begin to form in her eyes.
     “No honey...he’s not going to call back.”
     They stand embracing with tears streaming down their cheeks, in the open doorway, allowing the gusty breeze into their home that flashes occasionally with the blue-hued light of another brewing thunderstorm, breaking through the darkness and scattering about the hardwood that make up the living room’s floor.
