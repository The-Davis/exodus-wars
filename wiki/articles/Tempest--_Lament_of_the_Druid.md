This takes place early in the sixth year of the Tempest War

by Seth Dickinson

Arnold Wulfhar watches with hooded eyes as a storm of fire devours his home. Screaming waves of missiles hammer the forest; the overpressure from the explosions bends trees to near-ground level, as if they are bowing in surrender. 

Wulfhar doesn’t look away, even when the brightness of the blasts sends a spike of pain down his optic nerves. Howling winds rip at his clothing, and he shifts his weight unconsciously to avoid tumbling off his stony perch into the river below. The heat of the blast sears his sun-browned flesh, but draws no reaction. Years spent in the wilderness have sharpened his will; he will not shy away from mere physical pain.

The blasts fade like one of Centus’ spectacular sunsets. The lush forest valley below has been transformed into a scorched wasteland. Wulfhar’s dazzled eyes track the Prefecture bombers as they soar back upwards towards their carriers, trailing a long firecracker string of sonic booms.

He sighs deeply. The Prefecture will never understand the Centusi people in time to avoid a bloodbath. No, this…massacre is only the beginning. 

There are no civilians on Centus. Every single UCS citizen is a warrior through and through – even the children. Wulfhar wonders idly if he is the only exception to that rule. 

A curious traveler in the woods had once blundered into Wulfhar and called him a ‘druid,’ referring perhaps to his bulky, bear-like build and equally ursine lifestyle. The term had drawn an amused laugh. Wulfhar had once been an ecologist; he had departed on what he had thought would be a short field expedition, and simply allowed it to drag on for fifteen years. No mythical druid ever packed his hut with spectrographic analyzers, sampling equipment, and encyclopedias, but Wulfhar has painstakingly maintained his equipment and studies since his departure from civilization. 

Fifteen years alone in the wilds. Once, it would have seemed like a nightmare. Now it is simply my life.

Was my life.

Wulfhar is unsure of why the Prefecture has come to Mesar, but he accepts the reality without question; there is no longer any place in him for anger at something as inalterable as what is. 

Yet despite his acceptance, he feels fear, both for his world and for its people. Centus is a harsh place, but to Wulfhar that only enhances its beauty. The soaring, craggy peaks of Centus’ hundreds of mountain chains, the woods brimming with life despite the terrible extremes of climate, and the savage elegance of the predator-heavy ecosystem, are beautiful to him – but it is a beauty that will soon be permanently marred.

Every time he closes his eyes he sees the same nightmare images of the near future. Ancient, snow-capped peaks sculpted in the winds of countless millennia blasted into sand by orbital bombardment; fire devouring the woods and turning lakes into steam. Then, later, a patchwork of disaster: clouds of ash filling the skies. Volcanoes and earthquakes triggered by nuclear blasts. Huge swathes of woods gone brown and dead from lack of sunlight. Starved, emaciated corpses littering the parched earth – scorpanids, fenris, mammoth yak, spirit soarers, all victims of the human war. 

Wulfhar’s understanding of Mesar’s wildlife may be more complete than that of any other being alive. Mesar’s ecosystem has more predators than nearly any other world in known space; the plants on Mesar are guarded by mobile, deadly symbiotes, so even herbivores must be skilled at killing – and able to turn to another food source if the plant life proves too difficult. If the plant food supply is cut off by a lack of sunlight, there will be no large herbivores to sustain the predators. Catastrophic collapse of the ecosystem is the phrase that keeps dashing through Wulfhar’s head.

Like the mythical priestess Cassandra, Wulfhar knows he is condemned to be forever ignored. He has no power to change the course of this war, no power to save his beloved wilderness from the infinitely more destructive nature of mankind. 

But he knows there is hope. 

Mankind is like a wildfire, he thinks. And despite the destruction a wildfire may wreak, it is part of nature’s cycle, just like anything else. Seeds will spread on the winds, and the bodies of the countless dead will keep the soil fertile. 

Mesar will recover. In the end, everything is reborn. 

Smiling slightly, Wulfhar begins picking his way down the edge of the ravine and commences an ambling march towards the blasted ruins of his woods, determined to save what he can.
