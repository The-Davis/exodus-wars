&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Alekiev was not always as it is now, or so my father told me. Once, in the days of the Twilight Empress, before the Burning came to our world, Alekiev was prosperous. Alekiev was peaceful. Alekiev was home.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Living here is a daily struggle. The desert swallows people whole. If we try to leave alone, we die, it's as simple as that.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The wanderer was different. He came into town, wrapped in rags like some cast-off waif from the dead towns, but he carried himself like he had hope.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Have you ever looked into the eyes of people around here? They may be breathing and moving beings, but they're dead inside. It shows in the eyes, and in the stand. We hunch over, scuttling from one thing to the next, hoping fate doesn't rear its ugly head and strike us down like our fathers before us.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The wanderer walks tall and proud. The wanderer dares fate to strike him down. He entered our assembly hall, where we share the meager findings we can scrape together, and where the Meroniri handouts are doled out to us.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The wanderer entered cowled and cloaked, but soon revealed his face to us. His features were striking: bereft of scars and genetic flaws so rampant with us. No life-saving cybernetics marred his face. He had hair--a sure sign of an off-worlder--shoulder length and light brown, streaked with silver-white.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"May a stranger find a place at your table?" he asked us.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;How could we, who have so little, spare anything for an off-worlder, who has so much? Surely the elders would cast him out and make him leave us to our misery. But no, the elders nod one by one, and a chair is brought to our table.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;He eats in silence, but many of us mutter angrily. There is so little that this gift to the wanderer may mean the starving deaths of some at this table. When his pittance is consumed, the wandered stands, and casts off his rags to reveal clothing so fine that I have never seen its like. Indeed, it hurt to look on it, the gems were so polished. He spoke only a little before leaving.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;"I have supped at tables far grander than this, but they feed from their surplus. You, who have nothing, share it freely. Do not let this hope die."<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Then, in a flash, he is gone, and only a large sack in his empty chair shows that he was ever there. The elders snatched it up fast, but soon opened it up for all to see. We danced and sang for joy, for the first time I can ever remember. The stranger left us a water purifier, medicines, food that won't spoil, a shield generator against the radioactive winds, and a power generator to make them all work.<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;He left no name for us, but gave one solemn promise, written in our dialect: ''Lorithan will be saved, and I shall return''.
