Battle Armor is a combination of the heavy armor and weaponry of a Rig, and the inexpensiveness and high mobility of infantry. Of all war machinery, it takes the least training to master. The core of a Battle Armor suit is a fluid-filled shell of composite armor. The pilot sits in a fetal position in this shell. A system of sensors and chemical injectors plugs into the base of the operator's neck. Drugs are injected to temporarily deaden the nerves that control the operator's arms and legs. These control nerves are monitored by sensors, and redirect the electrical impulses into the motor systems of the battle armor. The armor's weapons systems are fired in different ways. Most armor systems have only one or two guns, in which case the operator "squeezes" his index finger on the appropriate hand. Some armor has shoulder-mounted weapons, which are fired by "shrugging" one's shoulders. If the armor sports a turret weapon, the operator aims by turning his head, and fires with a vocal command. The space between the outer armor shell and the core shell holds an energy cell to power the internal systems, and holds ammunition bins. A complex computer with communications, targeting and firing is also placed somewhere in this cavity, but it is so small that it can fit almost anywhere. Directly below the core shell, the suit has a series of gyroscopes that help to balance the armor in dangerous combat situations. Most of the time, the operator's inner ear and natural sense of balance will feed the correct balance adjustments to the armor's drive system, but in the event of disaster or complex maneuvers, the computer may take over to keep the armor upright and on its feet. If an armor suit has flight capabilities, the operator steers with head motions and vocal commands. Because humans can't fly naturally, there is no nervous system the armor can jack into to directly control flight. If a suit has articulating hands, the pilot must use a vocal command to turn off weapons triggering to operate them. Once this is done, hands work normally. The outer shell of the armor suit has composite armor, and pressure sensors that give feedback to the operator similar to tactile sense. The effect's intensity can be altered with vocal commands, and is usually cycled to the lowest setting in combat so the operator feels none of the suit's pain.
