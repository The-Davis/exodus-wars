The XB-2 refined the YB-1 to a point where it seemed to be useable. An XB-2 could carry two AWTs up to three Astronomical Units and return, with fuel to maneuver in between. The airframe was almost completely stripped to be able to save mass and devote mass to the AWTs and reaction mass for the Ion Drives. Unfortunately, the YB-2 had some issues with maneuverability in test runs: maneuvering thrusters were placed on multiple control surfaces on the wings, which had been stripped down to a minimum size. Despite the problems, the model was put into production as the B-2 Ocelot. By this time, the F-4C was prepared for escort. Matching the MPD system to the B-2 airframe proved problematic due to computer space needed for the AWT guidance systems, so it was determined that a new airframe would be used to incorporate the MPD.
