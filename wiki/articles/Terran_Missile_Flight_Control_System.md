Artemis Flight Control System - The Artemis is a suite of targeting gear, which is bonded to an individual launcher. A single Artemis cannot serve an entire vehicle if the vehicle has more than one missile launcher.

Most pieces of technology are pure weapons, but the Artimis IV is a work of art.It is born of simplicity, molded with ingenuity, and forged with fire. The Artimis IV FCS is designed to increase missile accuracy. It uses a simple system of infra-red lasers to get a lock on its target. The info is then augmented with recon data of the terrain, fed to the Artimis IV by the C3 networks from the recon VTOLs and grav vehicles. Finally, the missiles fire and hit their target 96% of the time. This entire process takes place in three seconds. Artimis IV FCS devices are equipped on Mechs and tanks that bear the brunt of battle, allowing them to take less time to aim. Many Rig pilots have lessened their firing time to less than a second, relying on instinct as well as the Artimis IV.

*Artemis I - The simplest control system. Also known as the TOW missile guidance system. A cable connects the launcher to the missile, and the operator of the launcher can make course corrections manually.
*Artemis II - Heat-based guidance system. Used most frequently by the [[Royal Imperial Air Force]] in the [[Second Exodus War]]. The Artemis II is now only rarely used.
*Artemis III - The Artemis III is a radar-based guidance system. It is now outdated and no longer used by militaries, but is used by some civilian security forces.
*Artemis IV - The Artemis IV is the most common Artemis series guidance system. It is a laser-based system, which will guide missiles straight to the target as long as the laser continues to paint the target.
*Artemis V - The Artemis V is the most powerful and effective of the Artemis series Flight Control Systems. It's based on Professor Hlow's gravity technology. Rather than generating a gravity field, it senses the gravity field of its specific target. The Artemis V is worthless near planets, so it uses Artemis IV laser guides as backup around large gravitational fields.
