Cloning has been feared and misunderstood ever since it was a mere concept. When frogs and sheep were cloned in the late [[20th Century]], the public panicked, envisioning bizarre zombies or soulless biological automatons.

The process of cloning is very simple, and simply results in a genetic copy of the original. The male zygote of a fertilized egg is simply replaced with cells from the desired. Cloning humans was forbidden for years, and when it finally became legal, it was surprisingly un-useful.

Cloned humans are simply like identical twins of their originals. Their fingerprints aren't even identical. The myths of "rapid-growth" and "accelerated-learning" technologies were quickly dispelled, and human cloning stopped simply because no one cared to do it.

In [[2300]], the [[Empire]] harnessed cloning technology for warfare. During their long return voyage, they'd developed artificial wombs for the unborn children of the crew, keeping female crew members of the return vessels at complete operational capacity. Using a combination of cloning and artificial fertilization, an army was created. The process was slow, and expensive. 

On average, citizens of Alpha Centauri--the only planet system under Imperial control in [[2300]]--were forced to take care of ten to twelve children for six years. From age six to fifteen, the army of the [[Empire]] was raised in training camps, and was trained in most forms of modern warfare. Today, most of the clones and artificially-created soldiers are still alive and serving in the armies of the [[Conclave]] and the [[Prefecture]]. Growing armies is too difficult for the [[Prefecture]] to bother with, and the [[Conclave]] has no civilian population to raise the soldiers in their early years. Cloning has had its fling, and will probably not be used again.
