What follows is the tale of the Siege of Lorithan and the War Against the Volucris, as witnessed by myself, Seil Helden nav Riimar, Herald and Skald of the Reigen Crown, and Advisor to Jiliur Beskolyda nav Raegos.  Though some may doubt the accuracy of my account, I seek only to relate the true story of our Lost Prince and what fate destiny conspired to weave in the distant Mirdek System.  Those who were present may disagree in their recollection, but those who witnessed it not, as I did, can dispute only in antipathy.  I seek not to build up the Prince by his noble birth, right bearing, or his deeds of valor and grace before the War, but rather to recount what I witnessed upon a dying world in a distant system, where the Light of Our People met his end.

And so begins the tale...

---

In the year 2333 of the Solar Reckoning, 3430 years since Raegos Direnson set foot upon Junheim, the plague that consumes worlds descended upon Lorithan.  Though her people fought bravely, they were hopelessly outnumbered, and so their king, the Prehkor, sent out missives for aid from all of the Great Alliance.

…And no help came.

Fighting on alone, the Lorithos called out to any who would help, any who would lend aid—who would save them.  Had providence never conspired to give our King a heart for compassion, or Raab's Curse anointed our Prince, such pleas might have fallen upon deaf ears when they reached Junheim.  

Alas it was that the King called the first Marshalling of the Great Houses in three decades, and summoned his allies together in a War Council at ''Ayar''.  

---

---

Before he first considered it, he knew it to be folly.  As his people’s plight worsened, still he did not entertain the thought.  Only in darkest despair did he earnestly countenance the scheme.  In Lorithan’s darkest hour, when all hope seemed lost, the Prehkor in his desperation, some say ''madness'', called out to the dark edge of the galaxy, to far Tolana...

...and he summoned forth the ''Warbringers''!

---

It was not long after that ''they'' arrived in the Mirdek System.  Tremendous warships, greater than any of the vessels of the Council’s Host, burst from the ether.  The innumerable Hive sought to oppose their entry, moving to block their advance on Lorithan, but even the utterly alien mind of the Otherness did not suspect their power.  

Yes, even the Volucris had forgotten them, their foes a millennium past!  But a thousand years of war has made the Ixoarchada terrible.  To my knowledge, never has so overwhelming a force suffered so grievous a defeat.  Though this first encounter is called the Battle of Point Loma, it was not battle; it was a slaughter, a cull.  The Volucris ''learned'', and for the rest of the War they did not again earnestly oppose the Ixoarchada in space.  Wherever the ''Kidas'' chose to go, they were given a wide berth.

---

In ''fire'' and ''steel'' they descended upon Lorithan.  The coming of the Volucris brought the darkening of the sun, but the Ixoarchada’s arrived was heralded by the burning of the sky.  Blazing blades pierced the heavens, their immense forms streaking through the air, charring their enemies in their wake.  They chose their landing places with the report of a cannon, and impaled the planet itself as they landed.  Before the Machine-Men even left their vessels, their footfall had been painfully felt by Lorithan.

---

---

At this the noble Jiliur arose rejuvenated, heart beating anew with the vita machina.  As the Adversary advanced, Jiliur stood with the Aetoni and took up Korfangr.  Reigenjord gleamed, though there was no sun in the sky.  He faced the innumerable horde, Motsander himself in the vanguard, and screamed his battle-cry.  There on the plains of Rikaz Jael, the Light of Our People strode into the Darkness, and made it afraid!

"Hızla odam Aetona, tanrılar bizi izliyor!", brave Captain Taşdovme called out.  "Stand fast men, the gods are watching!"  I know little of the the gods of Tolana, but even Raab himself must have marveled at the sight.  In ''fire'' and ''steel'' the Aetoni came to this world--how fitting that they should leave it in like manner!
