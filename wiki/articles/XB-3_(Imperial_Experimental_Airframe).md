The XB-3 went back to the F-4 airframe, but designed it for 95% atmospheric operations. It was meant to launch from a carrier, enter an atmosphere, deliver a bomb payload on the surface of a world, escape the planet, then land. Ideally, it would land on a captured planet-side base instead. Like the B-2, the XB-3 had its cannons removed. The XB-3 met all of its design goals, and when the YB-3 went into combat training and testing, it performed very well, able to dodge fighters and SAMs, and deliver its twelve 100kg bombs with pinpoint accuracy. The B-3 Gyrfalcon was ordered into production and produced for both the Royal Imperial Navy and the Royal Imperial Air Force. Only 150 were delivered due to expectations of a new bomber model to incorporate the features of both the B-2 and B-3 and utilize the new Magneto-PlasmaDynamic drive.
