'''Wernher von Braun'''
*Born: March 23, [[1912]]
*Died: June 16, [[1977]]
*Place of Birth: Wirsitz, [[Germany (Terra)| Germany]], [[Terra]]
*Place of Death: Virginia, [[United States (Terra)| United States]], [[Terra]]
*Allegiance: [[United States (Terra)| United States of America]]

''Wernher von Braun'' (born March 23, [[1912]]) was one of the leading figures in the development of rocket technology in [[Germany (Terra)|Germany]] and the [[United States (Terra)|United States]]. The [[Germany (Terra)|German]] scientist, who led [[Germany (Terra)|Germany]]'s V-2 Rocket program before and during [[World War II]], entered the [[United States (Terra)|United States]] at the end of the war.

He became a naturalized [[United States (Terra)|United States]] citizen and worked on the [[United States (Terra)|American]] ICBM program before joining NASA, where he served as director of NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center and the chief architect of the Saturn V launch vehicle, the superbooster that propelled the [[United States (Terra)|United States]] to [[Luna]]. He is generally regarded as the father of the [[United States (Terra)|United States]] space program. Wernher von Braun received the National Medal of Science in [[1975]]. He was tall, articulate and spoke English with a distinctive German accent.
