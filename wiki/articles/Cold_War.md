The '''Cold War''' was the period of conflict, tension and competition between the [[United States (Terra)|United States]], [[British Empire (Terra)|British Empire]], and the [[Soviet Union (Terra)|Soviet Union]] and their allies from the mid-1940s until the early [[21st Century]]. Throughout the period, the rivalry between the superpowers was played out in multiple arenas: competition of ideology, espionage, demonstrations of military prowess, industrial buildup, and the creation of new technologies in what would become known as [[The Technology Race]].

There never was a direct military engagement between the superpowers, but there was a half-century of military buildup, and political battles for support around the world, including significant involvement of allied and satellite nations. Although the [[United States (Terra)|United States]] and the [[Soviet Union (Terra)| Soviet Union]] had been allied against Nazi [[Germany (Terra)| Germany]], the two sides differed on how to reconstruct the postwar world even before the end of the [[World War II]]. Over the following decades, the Cold War spread outside Europe to every region of the world, as the [[Soviet Union (Terra)| Soviet Union]] sought to undermine the [[British Empire (Terra)|British Empire]]'s strength in its colonies.

There were repeated crises that threatened to escalate into world wars but never did, notably the [[Korean War]] ([[1950]]–[[1951]]) and the [[Soviet-Afghan War]] ([[1979]]–[[1989]]). Direct military attacks on adversaries were deterred by the potential for mutual assured destruction using deliverable nuclear weapons. The Cold War finally ended with the conflict known as [[World War III]], but none of the traditional adversaries of the Cold War were involved. Instead, it was the Muslim nations known as [[Arabia (Terra)|Arabia]] which was the aggressor, and the aid of the [[United States (Terra)|United States]] to the [[Soviet Union (Terra)|Soviet Union]] resolved the long conflict between the two factions.
