The extremely popular "SRM Launcher" was nearing the end of its useful life.  Prefecture commanders believed that the use of solid-fuel rockets could not last for long in an age of gravity-based propulsion.

Engineers have consistently failed to fine-tune gravity control, so a gravity drive for something as tiny as an armor-piercing explosive is probably decades, if not centuries in the future.

Development of "impulse engines" has progressed to the point where such a design works well with small ordinance, as demonstrated with aircraft missiles.  On an infantry weapon, blowback remained a serious problem, often knocking the trooper to the ground despite his powered armor.  The Army gave the design responsibility to the private sector.

BolTec Propulsion came up with the concept of copying the wildly successful breecher rifle round on a much grander scale. Instead of a gauss-launched slug, BolTec went back to the roots of chemical propulsion. The result is the BolTec Prototype 2 "Grand Slam" triple-stage Light Anti-Armor Weapon.

The housing for the weapon is a rather heavy contraption. It has a single launcher tube, along with a guidance computer. Without power assistance, a fully-grown man cannot run while carrying the weapon.

A "cartridge" rocket is easily loaded by hand. Initially, the weapon is launched using an impact-ignited chemical propellant, similar to gunpowder. Unlike gunpowder, the BolTec Compound-7 High-Temperature Ignition Propellant does not burn or ignite from heat.

Once launched, the warhead begins a timer countdown. It will not arm unless its safeties are first disengaged, or until the timer has calculated a minimum safe distance from the operator. At this point, a solid-fuel rocket kicks in. It has sabot fins for minor course corrections. Once the final course is locked in, a tiny impulse motor kicks in, taking the weapon up to its maximum speed of Mach-12. Obviously, the weapon requires sufficient time to accelerate. The average flight time is too short to even reach Mach-1.

The warhead is a timer-safetied, impact-detonated thermite compound. Without being launched from a weapon, the warhead can survive a fall out of a dropship without detonating. The timer is extremely safe.

All non-specialist infantry forces in all branches of the military use the weapon, designated the BT-2 LAAW.

Because of its extremely simple components, the weapon is easy to obtain, easy to keep in stock, and can be maintained by anyone who takes a simple one-week course. While a gauss/impulse/nuclear combination would undoubtedly be more deadly and effective, the Grand Slam does the job. Only one high-tech version of the BT-2 could be manufactured for every 20 of the low-tech version.
